Five points from this week’s adult sleep papers, and what they mean for UK practice.
A large obesity trial found that weekly retatrutide reduced breathing pauses more than placebo in adults who also had sleep apnoea, and it cut a great deal of weight. Weight management belongs with sleep apnoea care. The trial does not make the drug routine NHS treatment.
Among 12,715 adults in Turkish sleep clinics, about one in four had sleep apnoea plus insomnia symptoms, with more night-time breathlessness, morning headache and low mood. Clinics should look for both.
An online insomnia course used with 95 adults in routine care was completed by most people and linked with large improvements, but it had no comparison group.
Reports of insomnia trials show the usual programme covers getting out of bed when not sleeping, cutting down time in bed, and unhelpful thoughts about sleep, often over about six sessions.
Sleep apnoea questionnaires worked poorly in older psychiatric inpatients. Age divided people more cleanly than most scores, so a form on its own is not enough.
A small trial found that home jaw exercises and hands-on treatment both eased adult bruxism symptoms over eight weeks.
No paper on sleep-related painful erections was found. Child-only studies and animal studies were left out.
Only PubMed was searched, on 9 November 2026. Embase and Scopus were not searched, so those remain gaps.

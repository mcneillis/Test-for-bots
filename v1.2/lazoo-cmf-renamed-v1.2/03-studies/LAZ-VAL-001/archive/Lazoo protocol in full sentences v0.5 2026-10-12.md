## 2. Background and Rationale

People who keep a sleep diary, and the clinicians who later read it, need a digital log that captures the same constructs as the field's consensus paper diary and stores each entry with a timestamp, an audit trail and an export of raw values. The Consensus Sleep Diary–Morning (CSD-M) is the reference self-report instrument for prospective sleep monitoring (Carney et al., 2012). A digital version is not automatically equivalent to the paper original, because field names, the order of questions, derived indices and the way edits are handled can all change what is recorded.

Lazoo is software as a medical device at the Medicines and Healthcare products Regulatory Agency (MHRA) Class IIa (class 2a) working position, with one product layer. Its intended purpose is insomnia monitoring only, as decided by the sponsor on 13 October 2026, and it makes no therapy claims. Accurate, complete and auditable diary records are a precondition for any monitoring use of the diary. This study therefore tests whether Lazoo records the same night-level sleep quantities as a CSD-M completed at the same time, and whether adults can use the app for that task.

Calculated total sleep time is the primary quantity because CSD-M item 8 is a subjective estimate of time asleep and is not the derived index used for diary total sleep time in this study (see section 12). Reed and Sacco (2016) and Maich et al. (2018) show that total sleep time and sleep efficiency change if time in bed is measured from lights-out rather than from the later time at which the person tried to sleep. This study therefore keeps in_bed (the construct of CSD-M item 1) and sleep_attempt (the construct of item 2) as separate fields, and uses the formula in section 12 unless the Chief Investigator signs a documented variant following Maich et al. in the statistical analysis plan.

This is not a clinical trial of an investigational medicinal product. Whether the study is a clinical investigation under regulation 16 of the UK Medical Devices Regulations 2002 is a decision for the sponsor (see section 16). This protocol makes no statement about conformity marking. An artificial intelligence validation report and a clinical evaluation report lie outside this protocol.

Studies sponsored by other organisations lie outside this protocol, and their documents, letterhead, participant information and contact routes are kept separately from this file.

## 4. Study Objectives

### Primary objective

To estimate agreement between Lazoo and a concurrently completed CSD-M for calculated total sleep time over paired mornings, in adults using the restricted research app.

### Secondary objectives

1. To estimate agreement for sleep onset latency (SOL), wake after sleep onset (WASO), sleep efficiency (SE) and the number of awakenings, excluding the final awakening.
2. To describe completeness over the 14 mornings, in terms of paired mornings and missing data at item level.
3. To describe timestamp integrity and the completeness of the audit trail for edits made in Lazoo (who made the change, what was changed and when, with the original value retained).

The study will also describe the difference between calculated total sleep time and the construct of CSD-M item 8 (the participant's subjective estimate of time asleep, recorded as tst_subjective); item 8 is not the primary endpoint. ## 12. Endpoints

For this agreement study, calculated total sleep time is the primary study quantity and not a product claim. The two bedtime clock times are kept separate, and item 8 remains exploratory.

### Derived quantities (both diaries, same formula)

Clock times are dates and times on the diary_date, with the previous calendar day allowed for in_bed and sleep_attempt. Durations are in minutes.

- TWAK = out_of_bed − wake
- TST = (out_of_bed − sleep_attempt) − SOL − WASO − TWAK
- TIB = out_of_bed − in_bed
- SE = TST / TIB × 100
- sleep_onset (derived, not asked as a clock time) = sleep_attempt + SOL

The fields in_bed and sleep_attempt are always kept as two separate values. Naps and dozes are excluded from night-time total sleep time. Wake after sleep onset is the total of all awakenings except the final one. The primary measure of time awake after final waking (TWAK) uses out_of_bed − wake, rather than CSD-M item 6b. Item 6b (twak_try_min) may be used only in a pre-specified sensitivity analysis, if it is collected.

A paired morning can be evaluated for the primary endpoint when both diaries record sleep_attempt, sleep onset latency, wake after sleep onset, wake and out_of_bed, and these are internally consistent (out_of_bed ≥ wake ≥ sleep_attempt; sleep onset latency ≥ 0; wake after sleep onset ≥ 0; total sleep time ≥ 0). The field in_bed is required for the secondary analyses of time in bed and sleep efficiency and is collected every morning.

### Primary endpoint

The primary endpoint is the night-level difference in calculated total sleep time, Lazoo minus CSD-M, in minutes. It is analysed by Bland–Altman mean bias and 95% limits of agreement, with nights clustered within participant. The analysis uses no diagnostic cut-point and supports no claim that Lazoo is valid for diagnosing insomnia or any other condition.

### Secondary endpoints

- Bland–Altman agreement, and the intraclass correlation coefficient (ICC) by the method set out in the statistical analysis plan, for sleep onset latency, wake after sleep onset, sleep efficiency and awakenings_n.
- Completeness: the proportion of mornings with both diaries completed; missing data at item level; and the proportion of participants who are completers (at least 10 of 14 paired mornings that can be evaluated).
- Timestamps and audit trail: the proportion of Lazoo entries with both device and server timestamps, and the proportion of edits with who, what and when recorded and the original value retained.
- Total System Usability Scale score (0 to 100), and descriptive summaries of the three structured questions.

### Exploratory endpoints

Calculated total sleep time compared with tst_subjective (the construct of CSD-M item 8) on each diary, and the difference between the diaries on item 8. Lifestyle text and naps are used as covariates or descriptors only.

### Sample size justification

The feasibility floor is 30 completers, and this is not the final number. The signed statistical analysis plan will calculate the number of participants, with input from a sleep statistician, so that the 95% confidence interval around the limits of agreement for total sleep time is narrow enough to be useful. Until the statistical analysis plan is signed, the figure of 30 is described as a feasibility floor rather than a powered Bland–Altman sample.

## 15. Statistical Analysis Plan

### Analysis populations

The primary analysis is a Bland–Altman analysis of night-level calculated total sleep time (Lazoo minus CSD-M), reporting the mean bias, the standard deviation (SD) of the differences and the 95% limits of agreement. The primary analysis uses the paired mornings that can be evaluated, and clock times are imputed for it only if the statistical analysis plan defines a rule for doing so.

Regulation 2 of the UK MDR 2002 defines a medical device to include software intended for the monitoring of disease (legislation.gov.uk, read on 12 October 2026). Lazoo's purpose is the medical monitoring of insomnia, so it falls within that definition, and describing the study as monitoring only does not on its own take it outside the rules on clinical investigations. The inference is that notice under regulation 16 is still likely to be needed, for the same reason as for a diagnostic study: a device that is not yet UKCA-marked would be used by people so that its performance can be assessed for a disease-monitoring purpose.

On that inference, the likely steps are 60 days' notice to the MHRA, a research ethics committee opinion before first use, the MHRA's "no objection" letter, and any HRA Approval and confirmations of NHS capacity and capability. Regulation 29 is not the procedure cited here. The exception in regulation 16(2) for marked devices is not the route used, because Lazoo is not yet UKCA-marked. The MHRA's notes on its flowchart (23 July 2026) describe an unmarked device used for a medical purpose as notifiable, including where the output is not acted on, and state that data from a study that was not notified would not be usable as evidence of clinical performance for a later UKCA mark.

**Decision for the sponsor.** The sponsor will accept or reject this inference and record the outcome as the regulation 16 decision.

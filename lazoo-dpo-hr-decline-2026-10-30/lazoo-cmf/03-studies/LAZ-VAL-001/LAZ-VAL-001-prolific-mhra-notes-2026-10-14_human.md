### A6. Retention and deletion

Three different retention periods apply, and they are easy to confuse.

**1. Study data held outside the platform.** Prolific says that it does not keep this data. The sponsor sets the retention period, tells participants what it is, and deletes the data when it ends.

Privacy article, 11 December 2025:

> “We do not store any data provided within studies. These are carried out on external survey software/platforms.”

> “We won’t keep your data for longer than necessary.”

The participants' personal data article, 5 August 2026, requires the researcher's notice to state "How long you'll keep it" and "How participants can exercise any applicable privacy rights."

"Getting consent", 18 June 2026, requires the consent form to explain "How the data will be stored and how long it will be retained" and "How participants can withdraw their consent and request the deletion of their data."

Researcher Terms, section N, in the annex to the standard contractual clauses, tie the duration of the transfer to "your organisation or institution's retention policy" and to "no longer than is necessary".

**2. Prolific account data.** Inactive participant accounts are closed and deleted after 14 months.

Participant help centre, 26 June 2026:

> “For participants, this means that if your account has been inactive for 14 months, your account will be permanently closed and your data deleted. You'll receive email notifications ahead of this deadline — first at 6 months, then at 12 months of inactivity.”

The "Data Deletion Notice FAQ", https://participant-help.prolific.com/en/articles/449632-data-deletion-notice-faq, gives the same end point of 14 months, and says that the account history and the "About you" answers are then erased. The researcher privacy article of 11 December 2025 uses looser wording, "personal data is deleted within a reason period after account closure"; the 14-month rule is the specific one.

**3. Survey Builder submissions.** The researcher deletes them, or Prolific deletes them when the researcher's account ends.

Survey Builder terms:

> “The duration of our processing is however long you choose to keep those Submissions hosted in our platform. You can delete them at any time using functions of Survey Builder.”

> “If your account with Prolific is terminated for any reason, we will delete Submissions hosted in our platform.”

**For Sara to decide.** Prolific's deletion after 14 months does not delete the copy of special category data held by Sleep and Health Clinic App Ltd. The protocol and the participant notice need the sponsor's own retention period, which Sara sets. Prolific also says that demographic exports may disappear if a participant closes their account or withdraws a screening answer, so a later re-download cannot be relied on. Section N states: "We do not guarantee that this basic demographic information and performance information will be continually or indefinitely available, as its availability may depend on Participant account and consent settings."

## Part B: When the MHRA must be told

### The statute that matters is regulation 16, not regulation 29

**Legal requirement.** As smartphone software for insomnia, Lazoo is a general medical device if it meets the definition in regulation 2; it is not an active implantable device. In Great Britain, notification for a general medical device falls under **regulation 16**, in Part II of the Regulations. **Regulation 29**, in Part III, sets out the same kind of duty for **active implantable** devices, so citing regulation 29 as the duty for Lazoo would point to the wrong Part.

MHRA guidance often refers to "regulations 16 and 29" because a single flowchart covers both families of device. That citation is shorthand in guidance and does not move Lazoo into Part III.

Regulation 16(1), Great Britain text, https://www.legislation.gov.uk/uksi/2002/618/regulation/16, accessed on 12 October 2026:

> “Subject to paragraph (2), no person shall supply a relevant device (if that supply is also a making available of the device) for the purposes of a clinical investigation in Great Britain unless, before he does so, the manufacturer of the device or their UK responsible person has given at least 60 days prior notice in writing to the Secretary of State of the intended investigation…”

The notice must consist of the statement required by sections 1 and 2.2 of Annex VIII, read with Regulation (EU) No 722/2012, together with an undertaking to keep the documentation described in section 3.2 of Annex VIII for at least five years.

Regulation 16(10):

> “No person shall conduct a clinical investigation of a relevant device otherwise than in accordance with Annex X; and otherwise than in accordance with any conditions imposed by the Secretary of State…”

The manufacturer must keep available the report referred to in section 2.3.7 of Annex X. The text of Annex X itself was not read again for these notes, and the section number given is the one that the regulation prints.

Regulation 16(2) is the exception for marked devices:

> “Paragraph (1) shall not apply in respect of an intended clinical investigation of a relevant device that bears a UK marking otherwise than in breach of regulation 13, unless the aim of the intended investigation is to determine whether the device may be used for a purpose other than that in respect of which it was UK marked in accordance with regulation 13.”

Regulation 29(1) uses the same structure of "supply" and "60 days prior notice" for an active implantable device, and refers to Annexes 6 and 7 rather than Annexes VIII and X (https://www.legislation.gov.uk/uksi/2002/618/regulation/29, accessed on 12 October 2026). It is quoted here only to make the distinction between the Parts clear; it is not the duty that applies to Lazoo.

**What "clinical investigation" means in the statute.** On the text read, regulation 2 contains **no** free-standing definition of the words "clinical investigation". It does define **"intended for clinical investigation"** (https://www.legislation.gov.uk/uksi/2002/618/regulation/2, accessed on 12 October 2026):

> “(a) intended for use by a registered medical practitioner when conducting investigations of that device in an adequate human clinical environment; or (b) intended for use by any other person in Great Britain who, by virtue of their professional qualification, is authorised to carry out investigations of that device in an adequate human clinical environment.”

That definition sets out the category of investigational devices for the purposes of placing on the market. Whether it also limits the duty under regulation 16 is **not confirmed** by any case or by the MHRA. It is the main statutory basis for the argument, set out below, that a study with no clinician and no clinic falls outside the duty.

**Medical device and intended purpose: regulation 2, read on 12 October 2026.** https://www.legislation.gov.uk/uksi/2002/618/regulation/2. Great Britain revised text:

> “medical device” means any instrument, apparatus, appliance, software, material or other article, whether used alone or in combination, together with any accessories, including the software intended by its manufacturer to be used specifically for diagnosis or therapeutic purposes or both and necessary for its proper application, which— (a) is intended by the manufacturer to be used for human beings for the purpose of— (i) diagnosis, prevention, monitoring, treatment or alleviation of disease, (ii) diagnosis, monitoring, treatment, alleviation of or compensation for an injury or handicap, (iii) investigation, replacement or modification of the anatomy or of a physiological process, or (iv) control of conception; and (b) does not achieve its principal intended action in or on the human body by pharmacological, immunological or metabolic means, even if it is assisted in its function by such means…

> “intended purpose” means— … (b) in relation to any other medical device, the use to which the device is intended according to the data supplied by the manufacturer on the labelling, the instructions for use and/or the promotional materials.

Footnote markers on the legislation page are omitted here, and the operative words are those printed on that page. The MHRA's guidance on the health institution exemption, updated on 20 January 2026, quotes the same definition (https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/health-institution-exemption-for-general-medical-devices/general-information-on-the-health-institution-exemption). The statute page is the source used for the inference below.

### INFERENCE: notice of 60 days is likely to be needed, including where the study is monitoring only

**INFERENCE.** This is not a decision and it is not an MHRA finding on Lazoo. Sara, as sponsor, accepts or rejects it for sections 3 and 16 of the protocol.

The reading is as follows. If Lazoo is supplied to participants so that its performance can be studied, and the manufacturer's intended purpose remains the diagnosis or monitoring of insomnia, notice under regulation 16 is likely to be needed, at least 60 days before the supply. On this reading, heading the protocol "monitoring only" does not remove that step while the intended purpose remains the monitoring of a disease.

Two sources are combined, and no third source is treated as deciding the point.

**1. ### Software and insomnia, in the MHRA's own words

**Regulator guidance.** Software for insomnia falls within the MHRA's guidance on digital mental health (version 1.3, 29 July 2026), and two of its examples bear on the question of medical purpose.

> “This product supports treatment of poor sleep. It is only for well-being and when poor sleep would be considered at sub-clinical levels, rather than clinical levels as part of a diagnosable mental health condition.”

Example 2, which the MHRA says **has** a medical purpose:

**Opinion.** The Class IIa label applied by Sleep and Health Clinic App Ltd is consistent with that explanation of Rule 10 if Lazoo gives a lay user an insomnia diagnosis or decisive diagnostic information. These notes do not confirm any MHRA classification decision. The wording of Rule 10 on "direct diagnosis" is the MHRA's account of the Directive rule as it applies under the UK Medical Devices Regulations, and it is guidance. ### The in-house or health institution exemption does not fit Sleep and Health Clinic App Ltd

### The 60-day period, and ethics review running alongside it

**Legal requirement.** Notice must be given at least 60 days before the device is supplied for the investigation (regulation 16(1)). If, within 60 days of formal acceptance, the Secretary of State gives written notice of objection on grounds of public health or public policy, the device must not be supplied for that investigation (regulation 16(4)). The Secretary of State may instead give written notice that the device may be made available, or that it may be made available once the ethics committee has given a favourable opinion on the investigational plan (regulation 16(5)); that notice can carry conditions and can be withdrawn (regulation 16(6)). The ethics opinion under section 2.2 of Annex VIII need not be included in the initial submission, but must be sent as soon as it exists (regulation 16(3)). The manufacturer must tell the Secretary of State when the investigation ends, and must justify any early stop (regulation 16(11)).

**Regulator guidance.** Notify page, updated on 12 August 2026:

> “When we have received your documents and validated them, we will contact to you within 5 working days to confirm that the 60-day assessment period has started, or we will let you know if there are any issues. > “Day 1 of the 60 days is taken as being the first day that follows the date of acceptance of a valid application.”

> “We will send you a letter by the 60th day with a decision (‘objection’ or ‘no objection’)…”

> “If, within 60 days of formal acceptance of the Notice, the MHRA has not given written notice of objection, the clinical investigation may proceed.”

> “If the MHRA raises no grounds for objection … the investigation may only start once REC approval has been granted and a copy of the REC approval letter is sent by the applicant to the MHRA.”

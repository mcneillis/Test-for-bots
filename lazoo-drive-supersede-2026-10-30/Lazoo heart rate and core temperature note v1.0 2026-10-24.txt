The patch is now worn on the left side of the chest, about 20 cm below the armpit. It records heart rate over 24 hours, and heart rate is treated as a medical device function, not a wellness trend. Core temperature is estimated within the product from heart rate, using the method published by Buller and colleagues in 2013. Heartbeats are detected on the patch; heart rate and the core temperature estimate are worked out on the patient's phone, and the data leave the phone only when the patient chooses to share their data for a report, and the patient and their clinician then see the results in that shared report. Heart rate is hidden during pregnancy. How the core temperature estimate will be shown has not yet been chosen, and no core temperature figure, curve or label is shown to anyone until it has been chosen, validated for that use and checked again. The wider flow of Lazoo data starts only after the data protection impact assessment is signed off and the amended purpose is approved. Great Britain, Northern Ireland and the European Union are in scope. Lazoo itself remains an insomnia monitoring app. Its working view is Class IIa, in Great Britain and in Northern Ireland and the European Union, and that view holds only while every safeguard in the classification review is kept, including no live display, no alarms, alerts or thresholds, and no diagnosis of any condition, circadian rhythm disorders included. No class is final. The amendment to Lazoo's intended purpose and the data protection impact assessment are still being worked on, and neither is approved. The first design-around hold point (DA-1) stays in place until a patent attorney has reviewed United States patents US 12,426,839 B2 and US 10,702,165 B2. The earlier CBT-14 evidence and patent records are kept as a live record. Nothing else in this document has been changed.

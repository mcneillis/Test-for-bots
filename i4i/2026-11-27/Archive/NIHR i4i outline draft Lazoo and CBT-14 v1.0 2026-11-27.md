## Plain English summary

Sleep services still ask people to fill in a paper diary. The diary is easy to abandon, and the record is hard to audit. This project develops two things that stay within monitoring.

Lazoo is a phone diary for adults with insomnia. It is software as a medical device, at a working Class IIa position, for monitoring only. The clinician sees the record and makes any diagnosis. Lazoo does not diagnose insomnia or any other condition, and it does not offer therapy. The build in hand stores the diary and works out standard sleep measures by fixed arithmetic. Further help that would use artificial intelligence is planned, is not specified, and is not built. Marking for the United Kingdom (UKCA) and for Europe (CE), with a representative in the European Union, is planned around the draft 2026 amendment to the medical device rules. Neither marking is in place. The Digital Technology Assessment Criteria, the Data Security and Protection Toolkit, and the clinical safety standard DCB0129 are not in place. The statement of intended purpose, version 2.9, is not signed.

CBT-14 is the working name of a reusable skin logger, with a fresh adhesive for each wear of fourteen days. It is meant to record temperature and movement for the wearer to see and, if they choose, to share. It does not diagnose, predict, treat, or decide care. Its regulatory class is not settled. The company will build the prototypes. The NHS or university partner will not build them. The design choices that a factory would need are not all settled.

Over the award the company will run a validation study of the diary only. Adults will complete Lazoo and a standard paper morning diary. The study is about insomnia. Heart rate is not collected. Pregnancy is excluded. People are recruited through a research-volunteer service, not from NHS lists. The NHS site is still to be chosen. In the same award the company will design and bench-test CBT-14, using a freedom-to-operate note that is not a solicitor’s opinion. A group of people with experience of sleep problems will be paid to shape the diary, the logger and the words used in the study. That involvement has not started.

The lead organisation is SHC Sleep Solutions Ltd. A related clinic company shares directors and is not an independent supporter. The partner organisations are still to be confirmed. No one has been asked.

### Background and rationale

Word count: 1075.

Adult insomnia is usually recorded first on a paper morning diary. A paper diary is easy to leave unfinished and hard to audit. This award develops Lazoo, a monitoring diary, and starts CBT-14, a separate skin logger. It does not diagnose and it does not treat.

Lazoo is software as a medical device for adults, at a working Class IIa position under the Medical Devices Regulations 2002. The class is a working view, not a final view of the Medicines and Healthcare products Regulatory Agency. The intended purpose is insomnia monitoring only, decided on 13 October 2026. Version 2.9 of that statement is not signed. The clinician reads the record and makes any diagnosis. Lazoo does not diagnose, does not look for obstructive sleep apnoea, and has no therapy, including no cognitive behavioural therapy for insomnia.

The build in hand stores the diary and calculates standard sleep measures by fixed arithmetic. It has no artificial-intelligence function. Any later decision-support function will be written down before it is built. There is no training set and no model, so there is no independent validation of one. Marking for the United Kingdom (UKCA) and for Europe (CE) is planned, with an authorised representative in the European Union, around the draft 2026 amendment to the medical device rules. What that draft does to software class is to be confirmed against the final instrument. No UKCA marking and no CE marking is in place. The representative is not appointed. The clinical safety standard DCB0129, the Digital Technology Assessment Criteria, and the Data Security and Protection Toolkit are not in place. DCB0129 is the NHS standard for clinical risk management in the manufacture of health information technology.

The diary study is LAZ-VAL-001. The amendment says protocol version 0.5 stays in force until version 0.6 is approved. Version 0.6.1, dated 30 October 2026, records an approval note from that morning and also says the draft is not signed. Which sentence the team will rely on is to be confirmed. On the design, the versions agree, and heart rate stays out of the study.

Adults aged 18 or over complete Lazoo and a Consensus Sleep Diary–Morning at the same sitting, for fourteen mornings. The primary quantity is the night-level difference in calculated total sleep time, Lazoo minus the paper diary, in minutes. The analysis is Bland–Altman mean bias and 95 per cent limits of agreement, with nights clustered in the person. There is no diagnostic cut-point. The feasibility floor is 30 completers. The protocol says this is not a powered sample. A signed analysis plan will set the number. The plan is not signed, and the statistician is not appointed.

Recruitment is through Prolific, on the company’s account, not from NHS lists and not from patients of Sleep & Health Clinic Ltd. No device is posted. Actigraphy is out. Pregnancy is excluded. The product may still be used in pregnancy, by a decision of 13 October 2026, but this study enrols nobody who is pregnant. Heart rate was added in principle to the purpose on 24 October 2026, with no alarm and no threshold, and was kept out of this study on 30 October 2026. The research build does not take patch data. Ethics review and Health Research Authority submission are on hold. There is no ethics opinion, no notice to the Medicines and Healthcare products Regulatory Agency, and no decision under regulation 16. Insurance, the participant sheet and the consent form are not in hand.

The intended NHS site is University College London Hospitals NHS Foundation Trust, to be confirmed, with no agreement. A co-applicant from another organisation is required in either case.

CBT-14, in the design pack of 2 September 2026, is a reusable module and a new adhesive for each wear of fourteen days. ### Aims and objectives

Word count: 311.

The aim is to prepare Lazoo as a digital diary for monitoring adult insomnia, and, in the same award, to build the first CBT-14 prototypes at the company so that a later evaluation can be designed. The aim is not to diagnose insomnia, to detect sleep apnoea, or to deliver therapy.

Success for the diary study is the Bland–Altman description of total sleep time, completeness, an intact audit trail, and usability.

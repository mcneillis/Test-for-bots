## Which stream fits

The programme page says: “The NIHR Invention for Innovation (i4i) programme is a translational research funding scheme aimed at medical devices, in vitro diagnostic devices and digital health technologies addressing an existing or emerging health or social care need.” It says: “We fund innovations that have demonstrated proof of concept and have a clear pathway towards adoption and commercialisation.” It says the programme “has 3 funding streams”: Product Development Awards (PDA), Connect, and Funding At the Speed of Translation (FAST).

https://www.nihr.ac.uk/funding-programmes/invention-for-innovation

The same page says who may apply: “Higher education institutions, small and medium enterprises (SMEs), NHS or social care providers, not-for-profit organisations, local authorities and governments based in the UK (PDA & FAST); UK-based SMEs (Connect)”. It says what is funded: “Collaborative research and development projects for medical devices, in vitro diagnostic devices and high-impact patient-focused digital health technologies for use in the NHS or social care systems”. It says when: “3 researcher-led (PDA and Connect) and 2 commissioned (FAST) opportunities per year”.

The open call that matches a combined diary and prototype bid is the researcher-led PDA, reference 2026/510, inside the October 2026 PDA opportunity. The call page says: “This is i4i PDA’s main funding opportunity. The PDA researcher-led funding opportunity is open to any area of unmet need.”

https://www.nihr.ac.uk/funding/i4i-product-development-awards-pda-october-2026/2026510-2026511-2026512-2026513

The same October page is a two-stage call. It says: “To apply for the first stage you should submit an outline application. If invited to the second stage, you will then need to complete a full application.” It also says: “If your application is shortlisted you will be invited to submit a full application and present to the Committee.”

The other routes on that same page are commissioned, and this bid is not written to them.

- 2026/512 is for “applications to meet recommendations in research identified in NICE guidance that has been published or updated in the last 5 years.” The pack does not record such a recommendation.
- 2026/511 is for applications which “address any of the James Lind Alliance (JLA) Priority Setting Partnerships (PSPs) research priorities.” The pack does not name such a priority.
- 2026/513 is the themed call “Shaping and supporting the health, public health and social care workforce of the future”. The page says applications “are expected to focus on developing innovations shaping and supporting” that workforce. This bid is about an adult insomnia diary and a skin logger.

The October page also lists highlight notices (strategies and interventions for deprescribing, brain tumours, invasive lobular carcinoma, and traumatic brain injury). It does not say that a researcher-led application must address them.

Three other open or listed streams are a poor fit, on the pages retrieved today.

Connect. It does not list a FAST round closing on 2 December 2026. If a later FAST round sits on a part of that page the retrieval did not return, it was not seen.

The pack does not record a NICE recommendation for early use. The LeapX and early-use opportunity pages themselves were not retrieved today, so their money, duration and entry rules are not stated here.

Co-applicants “have responsibility for the day to day management and delivery”. People who only advise “do not need to be named as co-applicants.”

No character limit is stated for names. Organisation and department come from each person’s profile. No character limit is stated for those profile fields in this guidance.

Proposed role. The guidance heading is “Proposed role (300 characters)”. This role should be an adequately costed and resourced team member”.

ORCID. “In order to submit an application, all applicants are required to provide an ORCID.” No character limit is stated. The profile “will be used as your CV”. Visibility must be set to “everyone”.

Percentage of full-time equivalent. The guidance says this is the time each applicant will commit, and that time on concurrent NIHR projects “must not exceed 100% overall.” No separate character limit is stated. No minimum percentage is stated.

### Plain English summary

The guidance heading is “Plain English summary (3,500 characters)”. It says to write “a clear and easy-to-read summary”, “different from a scientific abstract”, and “Please do not copy and paste sections from your research plan.” If successful, the summary is used on NIHR websites. “Any claims or assertions made about the technology, including in the plain English summary, must have references provided.” That last sentence is an assessment rule on the October call page.

### Research plan

The guidance says “Research plan (maximum 5 A4 pages)”. The October call page says: “Do not upload a research plan that is more than 5 pages for the outline application.” It must be a PDF, “Arial size 12 font”, with “page numbers within the document footer.” The guidance says to download the template from the awards system, that there are three sections, and that “Section headings and margins should not be amended in any way.”

The three sections, in the form’s order, are Background and rationale, Aims and objectives, and Methodology/plan. No word limit and no character limit are stated for these three sections. The limit is the five pages.

The guidance says the background should cover the funding-opportunity aims, the problem, inequalities or under-served populations, why the work matters, and how existing evidence supports it. “We will only fund primary research where the proposed research is informed by a review of the existing evidence”.

The October page adds, for background, a health-economic case and timescales, a comparison with standard care and competing solutions, a detailed description of the technology, whether it is a medical device “including its class”, an in vitro diagnostic, or a digital health technology “including its classification”, the technology readiness level, “substantiated evidence of proof of concept”, and the challenges still to be overcome.

For aims, the October page asks for “key measures of success” and “the proposed project exit point, including target TRL, identifying end users and possible next steps”.

The methodology must also cover, from the domestic guidance: study management; working in partnership with those affected; knowledge mobilisation, dissemination and impact; the research team; inclusive research; and, where the programme requires it at outline, intellectual property or commercialisation. The October page does require intellectual property and commercialisation at outline. No separate word limit is stated for any of these parts. They sit inside the five pages.

The October page also asks the methodology to include work packages and milestones, “a combination of activities rather than focusing on a single aspect”, recommended approximate costs and ownership for each work package, risks and mitigations, the role of collaborators and subcontractors, and the named public-involvement lead. “A Gantt chart could be attached in the supporting information.”

Graphics may be embedded in the research plan. “Images may only be included within the 'Research Plan'. Images included in other sections will be removed”.

## Eligibility

### Lead organisation

The scope page says: “The lead organisation must be based in the UK.” Eligible organisations are National Health Service and social care providers, higher education institutions, small and medium-sized enterprises, not-for-profit organisations, and “local authorities, councils and other public sector organisations.”

An eligible enterprise has “a staff headcount no greater than 250 and an annual turnover no greater than €50 million, including start-ups, spin-out companies and subsidiaries (as per government definitions).”

The call page says enterprises “must be registered on Companies House with a UK address before applying.”

The FAQ says a new spin-out may lead, and that the same person’s time must not be costed at two organisations.

### Lead applicant

The outline guidance defines the lead applicant as the person with overall responsibility. No rule on the retrieved pages says the lead applicant must hold a particular post, degree, or clinical registration. The assessment criteria say the lead should be “suitably qualified and experienced” or have “appropriate mentoring arrangements.” No page retrieved today states a minimum number of years or a minimum number of publications.

### Co-applicants

At least two organisations. The scope page says: “Projects must include a collaboration of at least 2 organisations and may include 2 or more of the same organisation type.” “The organisation who owns the IP of the innovation should be involved.”

At least one co-applicant from an organisation other than the contractor. That is the call-page rule quoted above. The FAQ says there is “no limits on the number of co-applicants” provided control can be maintained, and “We expect a single collaboration agreement with all the collaborators”.

A co-applicant must have agreed. The call page says they must have an awards-system account and must approve being included. The FAQ expects discussions to have started. A contract does not have to be signed before applying. A letter of support is optional.

Collaborators abroad are allowed. The FAQ says their involvement “must be justified, in particular with regard to the benefit to the NHS or broader UK-wide Health System.”

### NHS partner

An NHS organisation is not required for every PDA. The scope page says: “If support for a clinical trial is requested, one or more of the partners must be an NHS organisation or social care service provider that will act as the site(s) for evaluation.” The FAQ repeats: “If support for a clinical trial is requested, one of the partners must be an NHS organisation.”

No retrieved page says that a diary study which does not request clinical-trial support must name an NHS trust. The different-organisation co-applicant is still required, and that co-applicant may be a university, another enterprise, a trust, or another organisation on the eligible list.

### Small and medium-sized enterprises

An enterprise may lead a PDA. The scope page allows “up to 100% of the requested funding, with no requirement for matched funding for commercial organisations.” The FAQ says: “There is no requirement for matched funding from SMEs. 100% funding is available for SMEs.” Connect, by contrast, can be led only by a United Kingdom enterprise, and it has no 2026 round on the dates page retrieved today.

### Technologies

The scope page says every proposal must involve a medical device or in vitro diagnostic “as defined by the Medical Device Regulations 2002”, or a digital health technology “falling under Tier C of the NICE evidence standards framework”, or, for real-world evaluation only, a non-regulated digital technology with “reasonable proof of safety and efficacy” that reflects Tier C. “You are expected to state the classification of your technology very clearly”.

The FAQ says: “Applicants must evidence in the application that their DHT meets and satisfies the Tier C criteria”. It says: “The i4i programme does not support DHTs that fall into either Tier A or Tier B”. Tier C, in the FAQ’s words, “covers DHTs for treating and diagnosing medical conditions, or guiding care choices and includes those with direct health outcomes, or that are likely to be regulated medical devices.”

For digital technologies the scope page also says: “Evidence that the NHS England Digital Technology Assessment Criteria (DTAC) have been considered should be demonstrated in your proposal.”

Applications that involve artificial intelligence must, on the scope page, describe the function, why it is artificial intelligence, the stage of development, any proof-of-concept data, any training datasets, whether it has been validated on an independent dataset, and the regulatory information for the intended use.

### What the scope page says is not funded

The list includes work with animals, animal tissues or stem cells; technology readiness levels 1 and 2; pharmaceuticals or cosmetic products; biomarker discovery; minor changes to technologies already in clinical use; “Scale-up manufacture of the product needed to carry out a clinical evaluation”; implementation costs other than those associated with research; evaluation of a product already adopted; Tier A or B digital technologies, including wellness apps; professional training, “including PhD fees and stipends, although costed time of the individual is allowed”; and innovation networks. Real-world evidence projects “must have regulatory approval or demonstrated equivalent safety and efficacy for the intended use.”

## Cost rules

### What can be requested

The scope page says there is “no maximum funding level” and “A minimum threshold may be applied during competitive rounds.” Enterprises and NHS organisations “may receive up to 100% of the requested funding”. Higher education institutions “are entitled to up to 80% of the full economic costs.”

The FAQ says: “Funding for i4i PDAs is uncapped. Typically, awards range between £0.5m-£1.5m but applications do not have to be within this range.” It also says: “applications below £500k would rarely be deemed competitive.” “PDA provides funding for 100% of research costs, normally paid quarterly”. For a higher education institution, “up to 80% of the Full Economic Cost will be paid, provided that the TRAC methodology has been used.” For “an NHS body or a commercial organisation, up to 100% of costs will be paid.”

The finance guidance is more precise for domestic programmes, and its rates section was last updated on 29 April 2026.

- NHS: direct costs “funded at 100% for all”. “Reasonable indirect costs: 0% for all”.
- Higher education institution: “80% FEC for all except Policy Research Programme and Mental Health Research Groups”.
- Commercial, charity, local authority, and independent research organisation: “Direct and reasonable indirect costs: 100% for all”.
- International: direct costs 100%, “Reasonable indirect costs: 0% for all”.

“The research or award costs are reimbursed; profit is not an eligible cost.” Payments “will be made to the contracting organisation only”. The contractor passes money on. “Appropriate sub-contracts or collaboration agreements must be put in place”.

The FAQ says there is “no limit to the amount paid to subcontractors”, and that the costs “need to be fully justified.” Subcontractors outside the United Kingdom are allowed on the call page “but suitable justification is required.” “Large industry collaborators may also be involved but they will not be eligible to receive any funding”.

“Earlier work, prior to project commencement will not be funded”. The FAQ says costs “can only be incurred from the start date of the project.”

Capital equipment “valued up to £5,000 excluding VAT” will be considered, in both the FAQ and the finance guidance. Items over that “will usually need to be leased”, with a lease-versus-buy note. The two pages do not agree about computers. The FAQ says: “The costs of computers are normally restricted to a maximum of £800 each excluding VAT.” The finance guidance says: “Computer costs are restricted to £1,000 each, including VAT and any software.” The FAQ tells the reader to use the finance guidance for the detailed view. Both figures are quoted so the team can see the clash. This note does not pick one.

Open access is not part of the outline total. It is added if the award is made. The finance guidance says schemes usually fund up to two international conference attendances for research of up to five years. Hosting a conference is not funded. Alcohol is excluded from subsistence. First-class travel is not funded. Daily subsistence allowances are not permitted. Individual items over £10,000 should be itemised. Output VAT on sales “should never be included”. Input VAT that cannot be recovered may be included. Inflation can be evidenced at contract close, not built in as an assumed pay award: “Exclude nationally agreed pay increases.”

PhD fees and stipends are not funded, on the scope page. Costed staff time is allowed.

NHS support costs and excess treatment costs are not part of the research award. The finance guidance says: “In England, support and excess treatment costs are funded via the Research Delivery Network.” The full-application part of the October page says the same, and that a Schedule of Events Cost Attribution Tool is completed “when considered necessary by the regional RDN AcoRD specialist.” If there are none, “you must explain why”. AcoRD is the NIHR name for attributing the costs of health and social care research and development. The outline uploads list retrieved today does not ask for that schedule. The finance guidance says the committee still takes these service costs into account for value for money.

Between outline and full application, the finance guidance says: “You should explain any percentage changes >5%”. The FAQ says that for “budget changes over 10%” the team should discuss the change with the programme before the full application. Both thresholds are on live pages.

Patient and public involvement costs, inclusion costs, and knowledge-mobilisation costs are eligible headings in the finance guidance. The cost of the involvement lead is a staff cost.

No page retrieved today states an industry match, a cash contribution, or a percentage the company must put in. The pages say there is no matched-funding requirement for an enterprise.

No page states a success fee, an equity take, or a royalty. The FAQ does say NIHR’s consent is required “before any Commercial Use of NIHR-funded IP is made.”

## Required partners

The minimum partnership, from the pages above, is two organisations, including the owner of the intellectual property, and one named co-applicant from outside the contracting organisation, who has agreed to be named.

An NHS or social care provider is required if the bid asks for support for a clinical trial, and that provider is the evaluation site.

A named, costed lead for working with people and communities is required. A separate public co-applicant is encouraged, and is not a substitute for that involvement.

A clinical trials unit is not required at outline. The FAQ says it “would be advantageous but it is not an absolute requirement”, and is “expected for larger clinical studies including Randomised Controlled Trials”, with a justification if it is not used.

No page says a patent must already have been filed. The FAQ says: “There is no requirement for having a patent filed or granted at the time of submission”. An initial freedom-to-operate opinion is required by the October assessment criteria. The FAQ says a professional search is “ideal” and “internal FTO searches are acceptable at this stage.”

## Assessment criteria

The assessment-criteria page says that an outline must address criteria 1 to 5, and “briefly indicate the approach” for criteria 6 and 7. The full application is assessed on all seven. “The application is assessed in its entirety against the assessment criteria; there is no specific weighting allocated to each criterion.” That sentence is from the PDA FAQ.

The criteria page also says the Research for Patient Benefit committee looks at value for money at outline “with regards to the funding tier”. That sentence is about that programme’s tiers. It is quoted so it is not missed. It does not, on the page, create a tier for i4i.

The seven criteria, in the print page’s words, are as follows.

1. Relevance of the proposed research. The research “fulfils a significant evidence gap and addresses unmet needs and priorities”, “is fully informed by previous relevant research”, “aligns fully with the scope”, “is relevant to the health and wealth of the nation”, and, for a commissioned brief, meets that brief.

2. Research quality and excellence. A robust plan, clear questions, detailed methods, a design that is “well defined, appropriate, valid and feasible within the timeframe and resources requested”, milestones, risks, ethics, and plans for management, governance and contracts.

3. Knowledge mobilisation, dissemination and impact. Outputs, outcomes and impacts; stakeholders identified; a plan “co-developed with key stakeholders”; and the activities and resources.

4. Working with people and communities. Who is affected, informed by lived experience; an equitable approach including “support, development, reward and feedback”; a plan for lived experience to shape each stage; the difference this is expected to make and how it will be evaluated; and a justified plan for managing and resourcing it.

5. Inclusive research. At outline, an evidence-based justification of the gap in health, care, inequality or under-served groups, and how inclusion was considered in the sample, the entry rules, and recruitment and retention. Sex and gender must be accounted for across the lifecycle, including the sampling rationale, any single-sex rule, and plans for disaggregated analysis. “Criteria can not be traded off against each other.” Some further inclusion items are marked on the page as full-application only.

6. Strength of the research team. A suitably qualified lead, or mentoring; “an appropriate range and depth of relevant expertise”; and interdisciplinary working.

7. Value for money. A “clear, well-justified budget”, expenditure that is “proportionate and appropriate”, and service-support costs that are “reasonable in relation to the likely benefits”.

The October call page adds three i4i points. In short, they ask the bid to show a significant advance over current products and a benefit to patients or the service; evidence for further development from work to date or the literature, with references for every claim; and an intellectual-property and commercialisation strategy, including an initial freedom-to-operate opinion, markets, third-party rights, partner agreements, adoption, and onward investment. If the technology is early, “adoption and exploitation plans can be one of the project’s deliverables.”

The criteria page says that in August 2026 NIHR began moving from a 6-point to a 10-point score. The October call page retrieved today does not say which scale this round uses.

## Supporting documents, gathered in one place

For the outline, the pages retrieved today ask for the online form, a research-plan PDF of no more than five A4 pages in Arial 12 with page numbers and the template headings left as they are, one single-sided A4 page of references, and at most one extra single-sided A4 upload, which may be a flow diagram or a flexible upload such as a one-page Gantt chart. A logic model is not wanted. A letter of support is optional and, if used, uses up that single extra upload.

The form itself requires an ORCID for every applicant, a conflicts declaration, acceptance of the lead-applicant role, and a yes or no on generative artificial intelligence.

The pages do not, at outline, require a signed contract, a patent, a Schedule of Events Cost Attribution Tool, or a clinical trials unit letter. The full stage, if the outline is invited, is a different and longer form. Its research plan limit on the October page is 15 pages, its reference limit is three pages, and a Gantt chart “must be uploaded separately.”

The template file name on the call page is domestic-programmes-outline-application-form-template.docx, last updated 28 January 2026. The download link was not captured as a separate address. It is offered on the October call page.

## 2. The fuller live wording: the draft of 12 October 2026

These passages are copied word for word from the draft intended purpose statement. It is a draft and has not been submitted.

**Medical purpose**

> Lazoo is software as a medical device. Its intended purpose is insomnia monitoring only: it supports a clinician in monitoring insomnia in adults aged 18 and over.

**Intended users**

> The intended users are adults aged 18 and over and the clinicians who assess and manage their insomnia.
>
> The patient enters the daily sleep diary. The patient receives no diagnosis, treatment or advice from the app and is not the user of its clinical outputs.
>
> The clinical user is a clinician who assesses and manages insomnia, in general practice or in a specialist sleep service. The exact setting will be confirmed with the final intended purpose. The draft Airlock form records the intended user as “General Practitioner; Specialist Healthcare Physician (sleep)”, and states that the patient enters the diary but is not the user of the outputs. That answer on the form is still to be confirmed.

**Patient population**

> Adults aged 18 and over with suspected or diagnosed insomnia.
>
> Lazoo is for adults and is not intended for children. Completion by a parent or carer, and use by anyone under 18, are outside the scope of the validation study until a participant information sheet for children exists.
>
> The intended population includes pregnancy: Dr Sara McNeillis confirmed on 13 October 2026 that Lazoo may be used in pregnancy. Version 0.5 of the validation protocol excludes pregnancy from the validation study because use in pregnancy will be evaluated later in a separate, dedicated study (sponsor decision, 13 October 2026). That rule belongs to the study and sits alongside the device’s purpose, which it leaves unchanged.

**Use environment**

> The patient records the diary at home, on a personal iOS or Android smartphone, which is a non-clinical environment.
>
> The clinician reviews the diary afterwards, in a general or specialist healthcare setting. The review may be remote, it may take place days later, and it may be carried out in a different service from the one in which the patient is usually seen. The degree of oversight therefore varies: some patients are reviewed often by a sleep specialist, and others occasionally in primary care.
>
> The app is not an emergency service. The validation protocol requires the participant information sheet to say so in plain language, and that sheet has not yet been written.

**What Lazoo does not use today**

> The current build presents the diary without interpreting it. The validation protocol requires the research-ready build to contain no interpretation, score, flag, CBT-I lesson or sleep-phenotyping output within the app. The fields reserved for core body temperature, wearable devices and AI events are empty in version 1 of the study build.
>
> Core body temperature (measured with the CBT-14 device), melatonin and other physiological measurements are not used. Wrist actigraphy is not part of version 0.5 of the validation protocol; adding it would require a protocol amendment, a named actigraph of appropriate regulatory status and a scoring procedure for that device. The clinical and AI description records a separately cleared actigraph only as a possible later input, if a later protocol adds one.

**Limits of the purpose**

> The clinician makes any diagnosis and every management decision, using the history and recognised criteria. Those decisions rest with the clinician and not with Lazoo.
>
> Lazoo gives no advice on any treatment, dose or therapy.
>
> The clinician makes any diagnosis independently, using the history and recognised criteria. Lazoo makes no diagnosis, assigns no diagnostic code and applies no diagnostic cut-off of its own, and the patient receives no diagnosis from the app. A proposed pattern label is not a diagnosis. A questionnaire total is reported in the questionnaire’s own published band, labelled as such, and is not a diagnosis. “Not interpretable” means that the diary cannot be read as a clear result.

**The short statement drafted for the artificial intelligence (AI) Airlock form of the Medicines and Healthcare products Regulatory Agency (MHRA), 1,127 characters**

> DRAFT, not locked. Lazoo is software intended for insomnia monitoring only. It supports a clinician in monitoring insomnia in adults aged 18 and over (ICSD-3 chronic insomnia; NICE Clinical Knowledge Summary). The patient records a daily sleep diary on a smartphone at home. Lazoo stores those entries and shows the clinician the diary and summary sleep figures calculated from it, so the clinician can follow the diary pattern over a review period they choose. The current build has no AI function: it is rule-based diary capture and fixed calculations only. Decision-support functions are proposed and are not built. No machine-learning function is claimed. Lazoo does not make a diagnosis, does not deliver CBT-I or any treatment, and does not assess obstructive sleep apnoea or other sleep disorders. It never reassures about breathing. Features that need a different assessment, including obstructive sleep apnoea under NICE NG202, are for the clinician to assess separately. May be used in pregnancy. Not for children. Software as a medical device, working classification UK MDR Class IIa, one layer; not yet UKCA-marked.

**The short patient population line for the same form**

> Adults 18+ with suspected or diagnosed insomnia, including in pregnancy.

**Classification**

> This scope decision keeps the working classification recorded in the source documents, which is UK MDR Class IIa with one product layer, and this statement infers no different class.

An older draft of 5 October 2026 described Lazoo as diagnosing insomnia and circadian rhythm disorders, with the patient as the main user. That wording was withdrawn by the scope decision of 13 October 2026 and is not reprinted here.

## 3. Proposed amended statement

**24 October 2026. Proposed, subject to Compliance.** This is the wording proposed after your decisions at 17:07 today. You approved the amendment to the statement of purpose in principle; it now goes to Compliance, and then back to you for approval of the final words. Until then, the current wording above stays in force.

> Lazoo is software as a medical device. Its intended purpose is insomnia monitoring only: it supports a clinician in monitoring insomnia in adults aged 18 and over. As part of insomnia monitoring, Lazoo may present trends in heart rate recorded across the whole 24-hour day by its companion wearable, CBT-14, so that the daily rhythm of heart rate can be read alongside the sleep diary when the clinician reviews the timing and pattern of the person's sleep. Heart rate is processed on the person's phone and leaves the phone only when the person chooses to share their data for a report; the person and their clinician then see the heart rate trends in that shared report. Heart rate is not shown in pregnancy, although Lazoo itself may still be used in pregnancy. Lazoo does not diagnose any heart condition or any other condition, does not analyse or classify heart rhythm, does not detect atrial fibrillation, does not issue alarms, alerts or notifications about heart rate, applies no thresholds that prompt action, and is not intended for monitoring in situations where a change in heart rate could result in immediate danger. Heart rate trends in Lazoo are not for clinical decisions on their own. Lazoo is not an emergency service.

Dated note, 24 October 2026 (Compliance round 4, R4-2): the statement above leaves out two of your answers of 17:07, the core temperature estimate (answer 1) and circadian analysis (answer 7). The revised proposal below includes them. It is written for display option C-A, timing only, which Compliance recommends; the display option is still yours to choose (decision R4-D1).

> Lazoo is software as a medical device. Its intended purpose is insomnia monitoring only: it supports a clinician in monitoring insomnia in adults aged 18 and over. As part of insomnia monitoring, Lazoo presents the person's 24-hour (circadian) rhythm, using heart rate recorded across the whole day by its companion wearable, CBT-14, together with the timing of the person's core body temperature rhythm, estimated from that heart rate with a published method (Buller and colleagues, 2013), so that the clinician can read the daily rhythm alongside the sleep diary when reviewing the timing and pattern of the person's sleep. Heart rate and the estimate are worked out on the person's phone and leave the phone only when the person chooses to share their data for a report. The person and their clinician then see these trends, looking back over the recorded days, in that shared report and nowhere else; there is no live display. Heart rate is not shown in pregnancy, although Lazoo itself may still be used in pregnancy. Lazoo does not diagnose any heart condition, any circadian rhythm disorder or any other condition; it does not analyse or classify heart rhythm or detect atrial fibrillation or other abnormalities; it does not show core body temperature as a measured value; it issues no alarms, alerts or notifications and applies no thresholds that prompt action; and it is not intended for monitoring where a change in heart rate or body temperature could result in immediate danger. These trends are not for clinical decisions on their own. Lazoo is not an emergency service.

### What the proposed wording does, in plain terms

The amended statement keeps Lazoo as a monitoring tool for insomnia and adds heart rate as context, not as a separate purpose. It reflects five of your decisions today. Heart rate is recorded over the full 24 hours rather than only overnight, because you said heart rate and core body temperature together are essential for reading the body clock. The working is done on the phone until the person shares their data. The person and their clinician both see heart rate in the shared report. Heart rate is hidden in pregnancy, while Lazoo itself may still be used in pregnancy, as decided on 13 October 2026. Northern Ireland and the European Union are now in scope, which brings in the European medical device rules there, including CE marking, a notified body and an authorised representative; the classification review sets this out.

The statement still says plainly what Lazoo does not do: no diagnosis, no heart rhythm analysis, no atrial fibrillation output, no alarms, alerts or thresholds, and no monitoring where a change could mean immediate danger. These limits are what allow the working classification of Class IIa to hold.

### Points the proposed wording leaves open for you

1. **Is core body temperature a claimed output of Lazoo?** You chose the 2013 method published by Buller and colleagues for estimating core temperature from heart rate. At 16:01 today, however, you kept the CBT-14 claim at skin temperature trends, with the core temperature estimate kept as a research output that is not claimed. The 12 October draft says core body temperature is not used, and the CBT-14 data-path lock of 18 September 2026 forbids naming or implying core body temperature. The proposed wording therefore says nothing about core temperature until you decide. If the estimate is shown to anyone, or shapes what the clinician sees, it becomes part of what Lazoo does and has to be claimed, validated and given stated limits of accuracy.
2. **What does reading the body clock mean for the claim?** The wording keeps the daily pattern as context for insomnia monitoring. It does not bring back the assessment of circadian rhythm disorders that was withdrawn on 13 October 2026. Please confirm that this is what you intend.
3. **The person now sees heart rate in the shared report.** The 12 October draft says the patient "is not the user of its clinical outputs". Showing heart rate trends to the person changes that sentence, and the final wording will need to say what the person sees and why.

Dated note, 24 October 2026 (Compliance round 4, R4-3): point 1 is answered. Your answer 1 puts the 2013 Buller core temperature estimate in the product, and it supersedes decision 1 of 16:01 to that extent. What remains open is how the estimate is shown (decision R4-D1) and whether it is also hidden in pregnancy (decision R4-D2). Points 2 and 3 are now written into the revised proposal (R4-2): circadian analysis stays context for insomnia monitoring and does not diagnose circadian rhythm disorders, and the person sees the trends only in the shared report.
4. **How Lazoo knows that a person is pregnant**, so that heart rate can be hidden, is to be confirmed.
5. **Timing for Northern Ireland and the European Union**, and who will act as authorised representative there, are to be confirmed.
6. Item 10 of your 17:07 answers is deferred until tomorrow.

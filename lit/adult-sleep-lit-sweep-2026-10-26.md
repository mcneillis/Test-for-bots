# Adult sleep medicine: weekly literature sweep

**Monday 26 October 2026**

A large drug trial is the main new treatment result: a weekly injection called retatrutide lowered sleep apnoea more than a placebo in adults with excess weight.

For a United Kingdom sleep clinic, the closest papers are that trial, a Spanish study of central apnoea after pressure treatment, a UK Biobank analysis of a broad sleep score and heart disease, and a cautious American guideline on shift-work disorder.

The tooth-grinding papers are a Brazilian survey at age 40, a 17-person German splint comparison, and a short experiment in healthy young adults.

Embase sent searches to an Elsevier sign-in, and Scopus stopped at the same publisher’s identity page, so this pack uses PubMed.

The figures below come from abstracts and are not advice to change anyone’s treatment.

This note covers peer-reviewed adult sleep medicine indexed from 19 to 26 October 2026, including adult bruxism and sleep bruxism. Obstructive sleep apnoea, insomnia and shift work are kept in separate sections. A single-author perspective on iron for restless legs syndrome is kept apart, because it presents no new patient sample.

Every figure below is taken from the PubMed abstract. Full texts were not used, so nothing here goes beyond what the abstract states. No citation was kept unless the digital object identifier resolved at doi.org on 26 October 2026 (each returned a hypertext transfer protocol 302 redirect to the publisher). Relevance is judged against adults in the United Kingdom, not only against people already inside a sleep clinic. Papers already appraised in the note of 19 October 2026 are not repeated.

## How the sweep was done

PubMed was searched through the National Center for Biotechnology Information Entrez service on 26 October 2026. The entry-date window was 19–26 October 2026.

Three searches were run. The first combined sleep terms in the Medical Subject Headings or in the title or abstract (sleep, insomnia, sleep apnoea, obstructive sleep, bruxism, narcolepsy, restless legs, circadian, hypersomnia, sleep disorder, sleep quality, polysomnography, and continuous positive airway pressure) with an adult heading or the word “adult” or “adults”, and removed paediatric, childhood, infant, neonatal and adolescent words from the title. This returned **123** records. The second searched bruxism, sleep bruxism, awake bruxism, tooth grinding or rhythmic masticatory activity in the title or abstract, without an adult filter. This returned **8** records. The third took anything entering the journals *Sleep*, *Sleep Medicine*, *Journal of Clinical Sleep Medicine*, *Journal of Sleep Research*, *Sleep and Breathing*, *Nature and Science of Sleep* and *Sleep Advances*. This returned **35** records.

The three lists overlap. Together they contain **155** unique records. Paediatric-only papers, animal studies, corrections, editorials, letters with no new results, trial protocols, preprints and a congress abstract booklet were set aside. The in-window adult set was large enough that papers with an electronic publication date before 19 October were not given a full appraisal. One record dated only as October 2026, with no day, is included in the parasomnia section, and the missing day is stated.

**Databases actually searched.** PubMed only.

**Embase** at [www.embase.com](https://www.embase.com) did not return a search. A results address answered with HTTP 403 from Cloudflare. A later request that carried the site’s own session cookie was redirected to an Elsevier sign-in path. The usual institutional route, Ovid at [ovidsp.ovid.com](https://ovidsp.ovid.com), showed a welcome screen asking for a username and was not searched.

**Scopus** at [www.scopus.com](https://www.scopus.com) redirected to Elsevier’s identity service (client SCOPUS) and that page returned HTTP 403. A results address also returned HTTP 403 from Cloudflare. No Scopus records were retrieved.

## Sleep-disordered breathing

Obstructive sleep apnoea and treatment-emergent central apnoea are collected here.

### A weekly triple-agonist injection for obstructive sleep apnoea

Grunstein RR, Somers VK, Azarbarzin A, Malhotra A, Sands SA, Lucey BP, et al. Retatrutide Once Weekly for Treatment of Obstructive Sleep Apnea. *The New England Journal of Medicine*. 21 October 2026. Online ahead of print. [doi:10.1056/NEJMoa2611477](https://doi.org/10.1056/NEJMoa2611477). PubMed 42904878.

*Findings.* Two phase 3, double-blind, randomised, placebo-controlled trials, nested in larger basket trials and funded by Eli Lilly, assigned adults with moderate to severe obstructive sleep apnoea and a body mass index of at least 27 to once-weekly subcutaneous retatrutide (4, 9 or 12 mg) or placebo. TRIUMPH-1-OSA enrolled people without type 2 diabetes (243 randomised) and TRIUMPH-2-OSA enrolled people with type 2 diabetes (448 randomised). Baseline mean apnoea–hypopnoea index was 58.6 and 55.9 events per hour, and mean body weight was 119.6 and 115.9 kg. At 80 weeks the primary comparison was the change in the apnoea–hypopnoea index with 9 mg and 12 mg. On the hybrid-treatment estimand, the mean changes with retatrutide in TRIUMPH-1-OSA were −22.9, −34.3 and −31.7 events per hour for 4, 9 and 12 mg, against −9.9 with placebo. The differences from placebo were −13.0 (95% confidence interval −23.0 to −3.0), −24.4 (−34.1 to −14.7) and −21.9 (−32.2 to −11.5). In TRIUMPH-2-OSA the changes were −19.2, −24.7 and −25.4, against −10.3 with placebo, and the differences were −8.9 (−15.9 to −2.0), −14.4 (−21.6 to −7.2) and −15.1 (−22.5 to −7.7). Intention-to-treat differences were similar: −13.2 (−22.6 to −3.9), −24.7 (−34.1 to −15.3) and −22.5 (−31.9 to −13.1) in the first trial, and −8.8 (−15.8 to −1.7), −13.9 (−21.3 to −6.6) and −15.0 (−22.5 to −7.4) in the second. Mean percent weight change with retatrutide was −12.5% to −22.2% and −12.1% to −18.5%, against −4.8% and −5.6% with placebo. The most common adverse events were gastrointestinal. The abstract does not give frequencies for those events, or the sex balance.

*Design and statistics.* Two placebo-controlled randomised trials with a prespecified primary endpoint at 80 weeks for the 9 mg and 12 mg doses. The hybrid estimand used a hypothetical strategy when an intercurrent event suggested treatment failure, and a treatment-policy strategy otherwise. Publishing the intention-to-treat result beside it is a strength, and the two readings agree. The 4 mg dose is reported alongside the primary doses. The abstract does not describe missing data, adherence or how many people were using positive airway pressure. A fall of about 14 to 25 events per hour from a starting index near 60 still leaves many people with substantial residual apnoea. Industry funding is stated.

*Population and UK relevance.* Adults with obesity or overweight and moderate to severe obstructive sleep apnoea, with or without type 2 diabetes. The abstract does not say which countries enrolled them. The first author is in Sydney. This body size and this severity are familiar in National Health Service sleep clinics. Retatrutide is described as investigational. The trials do not set United Kingdom licensing or National Institute for Health and Care Excellence guidance.

### Central apnoea emerging on pressure therapy, watched by telemonitoring

Díaz de Teran T, Correia S, Díaz-Lobato S, Muñoz-Cacho P, Ferreiro M, Ruíz Cubillán JJ, et al. Early detection of emergent central apnoea in patients on positive airway pressure therapy: A five-year telemonitoring cohort study. *Pulmonology*. Electronic publication 23 October 2026; December 2026;32(1):2742741. [doi:10.1080/25310429.2026.2742741](https://doi.org/10.1080/25310429.2026.2742741). PubMed 42922384.

*Findings.* In 4,021 people with obstructive sleep apnoea treated with positive airway pressure and telemonitored from 2017 to 2022, treatment-emergent central sleep apnoea occurred in 171 (4.3%, 95% confidence interval 3.7–4.9). It was defined as a residual apnoea–hypopnoea index above 15 with at least half of the events central. People with an obstructive apnoea index above 5, or a central apnoea index of 5 or more at baseline, were excluded. Most cases were prolonged, lasting 12 to 24 weeks (90.6%); 5.9% were transient (under 12 weeks) and 3.5% were persistent (still present after 24 weeks). Onset was usually within the first 60 days. In a nested case-control analysis, older age (odds ratio 1.03, p < 0.001) and male sex (odds ratio 2.35, p = 0.003) predicted the finding. The abstract does not give a confidence interval for those two odds ratios, or the mean age and sex balance of the 4,021.

*Design and statistics.* Retrospective cohort with Kaplan–Meier time-to-onset and a nested case-control comparison. The definition is operational and will move if the residual-index threshold moves. Excluding people who still had a high obstructive index focuses the label on central events, and it also drops a mixed picture that clinics see often. Treatment changes during follow-up may themselves have shaped how long the pattern lasted, which the authors note. An odds ratio of 1.03 per year of age is a small shift on the probability scale; the male-sex ratio is larger. The abstract does not say how missing telemonitoring nights were handled.

*Population and UK relevance.* The affiliation is a sleep and non-invasive ventilation unit in Santander, Spain. Pressure therapy with remote monitoring is routine in the National Health Service, and a figure of about four in a hundred, clustered in the first two months, is a service question for follow-up rather than a new diagnostic test. Age structure, sex mix and the devices in use may differ from a British clinic. The abstract does not estimate a United Kingdom rate.

### When the event count and the oxygen-desaturation count disagree

Ehab A, Abdelwahab HW, Hamoda M, Elhosiny MM. Apnea-hypopnea index-oxygen desaturation index (AHI-ODI) discordance in obstructive sleep apnea: association with symptom burden and cardiometabolic comorbidity: A retrospective cross-sectional study. *Respiratory Medicine*. 20 October 2026;264:109237. [doi:10.1016/j.rmed.2026.109237](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.rmed.2026.109237). PubMed 42901132.

*Findings.* In 200 adults with obstructive sleep apnoea seen from January 2023 to January 2025, the gap between the apnoea–hypopnoea index and the oxygen desaturation index was examined against excessive daytime sleepiness, defined as an Epworth Sleepiness Scale score of at least 11. The cohort was 77.5% male, with a median age of 57 years (48.8–64.2). For sleepiness, that gap had the highest area under the curve (0.639, p < 0.001), ahead of time below 90% saturation (0.627) and mean saturation and the desaturation index (0.609 each). The apnoea–hypopnoea index itself was not discriminative (0.546, p = 0.264). No pairwise difference between these areas was significant. After adjustment for age, sex, body mass index and a sex interaction, the gap remained associated with sleepiness (odds ratio 1.04 per event per hour, 95% confidence interval 1.01–1.07, p = 0.008), with no significant sex interaction. For diabetes and hypertension the highest areas were for time below 90% saturation (0.644 and 0.615), and for heart failure the highest was mean saturation (0.608). No index discriminated previous stroke or atrial fibrillation. The authors say discrimination was insufficient, the cut-offs are exploratory, and the gap is not a measure of sleep-apnoea hypoxic burden.

*Design and statistics.* Single-centre retrospective cross-sectional study. Five indices were compared without a pre-stated winner, and the “highest” area under the curve was not significantly higher than the others. An odds ratio of 1.04 per event per hour is a small change in odds. Areas around 0.61 to 0.64 are weak classifiers. The abstract does not describe missing oximetry or how many people were already on treatment.

*Population and UK relevance.* A single-centre series. The corresponding author is affiliated with Mansoura University in Egypt and a lung centre in Hemer, Germany, and the abstract does not say which site supplied the 200 records. The practical point that travels is modest: the event rate and the desaturation rate are not the same number, and neither separated sleepy from non-sleepy patients well in this sample. These areas are not a reporting standard for United Kingdom laboratories.

### A hand span instead of a tape measure in the NoSAS score

Kassir S, Rabec C, Heinzer R, Von Garnier C, Latreche L, Hajji S, et al. Patient-Performed Neck Encirclement Within the NoSAS Score: Score Availability and Diagnostic Performance in a Tertiary Sleep Centre. *Nature and Science of Sleep*. Electronic publication 19 October 2026;18:646897. [doi:10.2147/NSS.S646897](https://doi.org/10.2147/NSS.S646897). PubMed 42921212.

*Findings.* The NoSAS score (neck circumference, obesity, snoring, age and sex) uses a neck circumference above 40 cm. In 449 consecutive patients referred for suspected obstructive sleep apnoea in Lyon between September and December 2025, a patient-performed hand encirclement of the neck was available more often than a measured circumference (94.0% versus 71.3%). A score that used the hand method could be calculated more often than the standard score (91.3% versus 68.8%). In 305 people with both measures, the difference in sensitivity at an apnoea–hypopnoea index of at least 15 per hour was 0.0 percentage points (95% confidence interval −1.9 to 1.9), and the difference in specificity was +1.0 (−2.0 to 5.1). At an index of at least 30 the differences were 0.0 (−2.5 to 2.5) and +0.5 (−1.6 to 2.7). The abstract does not give age or sex.

*Design and statistics.* Retrospective analysis of prospectively collected routine data, with paired complete-case comparisons and McNemar tests. The confidence intervals around the sensitivity difference are tight and centred on zero, which supports a claim of little difference in this sample. Specificity intervals are wider and also include zero. People without a tape-measure reading are absent from the paired comparison, so the performance claim is for the subset in whom both methods could be done. The gain in the share of people who receive a score is the clearer result. The abstract does not give the sensitivity and specificity of either score on its own, only the differences.

*Population and UK relevance.* A tertiary sleep centre in Lyon, which is closer to a National Health Service referral clinic than a community screen. The hand method is an equipment-free substitute for a tape measure inside one existing score. It has not been checked here against British referral patterns, and the abstract does not propose it as a replacement for a sleep study.

### Eight weeks of resistance training and questionnaire sleep in severe apnoea

de Almeida JRV, da Silva Ferreira DK, de Arantes Lima VD, Dos Santos ENN, Dos Santos de Oliveira Silva NS, Aguiar JLP, et al. Effects of resistance training on subjective sleep quality, excessive daytime sleepiness, and quality of life in patients with obstructive sleep apnea: a secondary analysis from a randomized controlled trial. *Sleep and Breathing*. 20 October 2026;30(6):296. [doi:10.1007/s11325-026-03842-5](https://doi.org/10.1007/s11325-026-03842-5). PubMed 42900248.

*Findings.* Twenty-five adults with moderate to severe obstructive sleep apnoea (mean age 54.6 years, standard deviation 7.2; body mass index 36.1, standard deviation 7.9; apnoea–hypopnoea index 52.2, standard deviation 27.4) were randomised to resistance training three times a week or to a control described as two sessions a week, for eight weeks. On the Pittsburgh Sleep Quality Index, the training group changed by −3.2 points (standard deviation 0.6) and the control group by +0.2 (standard deviation 0.8), p = 0.004. Component differences were also reported for sleep latency (−1.0 versus +0.2, p = 0.018), sleep duration (−0.6 versus −0.2, p = 0.012) and sleeping medication (−0.2 versus +0.8, p = 0.007). On the 36-item Short Form health survey, emotional-role scores changed by +29.6 versus −17.9 (p = 0.008) and the global score by +10.8 versus −3.4 (p = 0.002). The Epworth Sleepiness Scale did not differ. The abstract does not give the sex balance, the number in each arm, or what the control sessions consisted of. The Pittsburgh changes are score points, not minutes.

*Design and statistics.* Secondary analysis of a randomised trial, with generalised estimating equations. Twenty-five people, split into two arms, is a small base for several questionnaire domains. The abstract does not say that the Pittsburgh total score was a prespecified secondary endpoint of the parent trial, and it does not mention drop-out or missing questionnaires. A three-point move on that index is large for eight weeks of training and should be read beside the missing description of the control. Sleepiness, which is often what brings people to a clinic, did not differ. No sleep-study outcome is reported in this analysis.

*Population and UK relevance.* Adults in Recife, Brazil, with obesity and severe apnoea on average. Exercise is already discussed in British clinics as part of weight and cardiovascular care. This abstract is too small, and too unclear about the control, to show that resistance training improves sleep in National Health Service patients.

### Frontal slow-wave balance on the diagnostic night and the pressure-titration night

Yazici DA, Bilensoy E, Gul E, Bahadir A, Yurt S, Ozgul MA, et al. Frontal Slow-Wave Asymmetry in Obstructive Sleep Apnea and Its Modulation During CPAP Titration. *Sleep*. 22 October 2026. Advance article. [doi:10.1093/sleep/zsag280](https://doi.org/10.1093/sleep/zsag280). PubMed 42911188.

*Findings.* In 143 adults who had a diagnostic polysomnogram and, on a later night, a continuous positive airway pressure titration (median age 47 years, 76% male), the median apnoea–hypopnoea index fell from 27.4 to 3.2 events per hour (p < 0.001). Frontal delta power in stage N3 fell at both F3 and F4 (p < 0.001). The absolute asymmetry index fell from 9.35% to 7.55% (p = 0.008). Signed asymmetry shifted toward left-sided dominance (p < 0.001). Right-dominant patterns were more common on the diagnostic night and left-dominant patterns on treatment. Among people with non-neutral asymmetry on both nights, the direction of dominance changed (McNemar p = 0.008), mainly from right to left.

*Design and statistics.* Retrospective paired comparison with nonparametric tests. The order of nights is fixed, diagnostic then titration, so a second-night effect cannot be separated from the effect of pressure. The authors say the findings should be read with night-to-night variation in mind. There is no untreated second-night control. The asymmetry change is about two percentage points. The abstract does not adjust for how completely the apnoea was abolished, or for sleep position and sleep stage mix beyond the use of stage N3.

*Population and UK relevance.* Pulmonary-sleep patients in Istanbul who were already booked for titration. The age and the male majority resemble many British treatment lists. The observation is about the electroencephalogram under pressure, not about symptoms or cardiovascular outcomes, and it does not change how a United Kingdom laboratory scores a study.

### Obstructive sleep apnoea in people with fatigue after COVID-19

Bawa AP, Lopez M, Miller J, Sala MA, Maas MB, Liotta EM, et al. Obstructive sleep apnea is frequent in patients with Long COVID fatigue but is not significantly associated with alterations of quality of life and cognition. *GeroScience*. 20 October 2026. Online ahead of print. [doi:10.1007/s11357-026-02604-1](https://doi.org/10.1007/s11357-026-02604-1). PubMed 42897409.

*Findings.* One hundred consecutive patients at a neuro-COVID clinic, with fatigue after COVID-19 that had not required hospital admission and with no prior diagnosis of obstructive sleep apnoea, had a home sleep test. Mean age was 46.4 years and 70% were women. Apnoea was present in 53%, of whom 33% of the whole sample had mild disease (apnoea–hypopnoea index 5–14.9) and 20% had moderate to severe disease (index at least 15). Male sex, older age and higher body mass index were associated with apnoea. Symptom burden, quality of life and objective cognitive scores did not differ significantly by apnoea status. In multiple linear regression, neither the apnoea–hypopnoea index nor the mean desaturation nadir predicted quality of life or cognitive scores. The authors describe 53% as nearly twice the prevalence in the general adult population of the United States, and they note that the one in five with moderate to severe disease is clinically important for cardiopulmonary risk.

*Design and statistics.* Consecutive clinic series with home, not in-laboratory, testing. “No significant difference” is not a demonstration that apnoea and cognition are unrelated; the abstract does not give the confidence intervals around those comparisons. The sample is selected for fatigue after non-hospitalised infection, so the 53% is not a prevalence for everyone with a COVID-19 history. Prior undiagnosed apnoea cannot be separated from apnoea that followed the infection.

*Population and UK relevance.* A single neurology clinic in Chicago. Long COVID clinics in the National Health Service see a similar symptom cluster, in a population that is on average younger and more female than a typical sleep-apnoea list. The 53% figure is not a British prevalence. The useful clinic point is the share with at least moderate apnoea, which the authors flag as worth treating for its own sake, whether or not it explains the fatigue.

## Insomnia and short sleep

### Stellate ganglion block plus press needles for a traditional insomnia pattern

Li X, Zhang X, Chen Y, Yue J, Cao X, Yu T, et al. Efficacy of Stellate Ganglion Block Combined with Press-Needle Therapy in Patients with Insomnia Disorder of Heart-Spleen Deficiency Pattern: A Randomized Sham-Controlled Trial. *Nature and Science of Sleep*. Electronic publication 19 October 2026;18:633039. [doi:10.2147/NSS.S633039](https://doi.org/10.2147/NSS.S633039). PubMed 42921502.

*Findings.* Seventy-two adults with insomnia disorder (International Classification of Sleep Disorders, third edition), a Pittsburgh Sleep Quality Index of at least 10, and a heart–spleen deficiency pattern were randomised. Everyone received an ultrasound-guided stellate ganglion block once a day for ten days. The randomised contrast was active versus sham press-needle therapy. Eight people were excluded for incomplete follow-up, leaving 64 (32 in each group). After treatment, median Pittsburgh scores were 12.00 (10.00, 14.00) with active needles and 14.00 (11.00, 15.00) with sham. The Hodges–Lehmann median difference was −2.00 points (95% confidence interval −3.00 to 0.00; Mann–Whitney U = 340.50; p = 0.020). At two weeks the scores were 9.00 versus 12.00, mean difference −3.56 (−5.18 to −1.94; p < 0.001). Depression scores improved more with active needles; anxiety scores did not differ significantly. Serum cortisol fell in the active group and rose in the sham group. Wearable total sleep time showed an overall group difference, but the group-by-time interaction was not significant. No serious adverse events occurred. The abstract does not give age or sex. PubMed also tags the record as a case report, which does not match the trial design in the abstract.

*Design and statistics.* Single-centre randomised sham-controlled trial of the needle adjunct, not of the nerve block, because both arms received the block. Dropping eight people with incomplete data is a complete-case analysis, not an intention-to-treat analysis. The immediate median difference has a confidence interval that reaches zero while the rank-test p value is 0.020, so that first contrast is small and statistically fragile. The two-week mean difference excludes zero and is the firmer subjective result. Objective sleep did not show a significant group-by-time interaction. A traditional pattern label limits who the result applies to.

*Population and UK relevance.* Adults in a Chinese-medicine anaesthetics department in Guangzhou, selected for a pattern that is not a National Health Service diagnosis. The design cannot say whether the nerve block itself helps insomnia. It is not evidence for offering this combination in routine British practice.

### Adding a sleep extension programme to lifestyle advice in prediabetes

Malone SK, Patterson F, Hu J, Goyal C, Melkus GD. Randomized Controlled Trial of Lifestyle Plus Sleep Extension Intervention on Adults With Prediabetes and Short Sleep. *Nursing Research*. 22 October 2026. Online ahead of print. [doi:10.1097/NNR.0000000000000957](https://doi.org/10.1097/NNR.0000000000000957). PubMed 42910621.

*Findings.* Adults with prediabetes confirmed by glycated haemoglobin, and short sleep confirmed by actigraphy, were randomised to an eight-week lifestyle programme or the same programme plus a behavioural sleep intervention, delivered one to one by video. Among 196 participants there was no significant between-group difference in the primary outcome, the percentage of time sensor glucose was above 140 mg/dL. Both groups increased their sleep and lost weight, with no significant between-group difference for either. Self-reported sleep quality and daytime sleepiness improved more in the sleep arm. Sleep onset moved earlier in the sleep arm only, and the between-group difference for that shift was not significant. In a post hoc analysis, an earlier sleep onset was associated with greater weight loss regardless of group, and the change in sleep duration was not. The abstract gives no effect sizes, confidence intervals or exact p values, and it does not state age, sex or country. The video platform is described as meeting a United States health-privacy law.

*Design and statistics.* Two-arm randomised trial with continuous glucose monitoring and actigraphy, analysed as a modified intention-to-treat comparison. The null primary result is the main finding, and it is reported without the size of the glucose difference, so a reader cannot see whether the trial excluded a worthwhile effect or was simply imprecise. Within-group improvements in both arms are compatible with the shared lifestyle programme, with time, and with taking part in a study. The sleep-timing finding is post hoc. The abstract does not say how missing sensor or actigraphy data were handled. The authors report no conflicts of interest.

*Population and UK relevance.* Adults with prediabetes and short sleep, seen twice in clinic and otherwise at home. Where they live is not stated. The question, whether teaching people to sleep longer improves glucose beyond ordinary lifestyle advice, is relevant to National Health Service diabetes prevention. This abstract’s answer on glucose is null, and it does not show that the sleep programme outperformed lifestyle advice on weight or on actigraphic sleep.

### Supervised walking and sleep quality on a psychiatric ward

Khoshnoud Speily S, Mansour-Ghanaei R, Soleimani R, Maroufizadeh S. Effect of a Researcher-Supervised Walking Program With Smartphone-Based Step Monitoring on Sleep Quality in Older Psychiatric Inpatients: A Randomized Controlled Trial. *Health Science Reports*. Electronic publication 23 October 2026; November 2026;9(11):e73413. [doi:10.1002/hsr2.73413](https://doi.org/10.1002/hsr2.73413). PubMed 42920831.

*Findings.* Sixty-two older adults admitted to a psychiatric hospital in Guilan, Iran, were randomised to routine care or to routine care plus four weeks of researcher-supervised outdoor walking, 30 minutes three times a week, with smartphone step counts. Sleep was the Pittsburgh Sleep Quality Index. Complete scores were available for 55 people. Mean scores fell from 12.93 (standard deviation 3.94) to 5.89 (3.48) in the walking group and from 11.64 (3.94) to 9.39 (3.07) in the control group. The between-group difference in change was −4.79 points (95% confidence interval −6.05 to −3.52), Cohen’s d = 2.09 (1.44–2.77). The abstract does not give a numeric age range or the sex balance.

*Design and statistics.* Randomised trial. The prespecified analysis was analysis of covariance, and the authors say its assumptions were not fully met, so they used a post hoc Welch test on change scores. That is a real change of plan, even though the change-score result is reported with a confidence interval. Seven of 62 people are missing from the analysis, with no imputation. An effect size above 2, over four weeks, in people who are in hospital and walked with a researcher, is large enough to need replication under less supervised conditions. The outcome is a questionnaire, not a sleep study. Blinding is not described, and a walking programme is hard to blind.

*Population and UK relevance.* Older psychiatric inpatients in one Iranian hospital. National Health Service mental-health wards also contend with poor sleep. A supervised outdoor programme with a researcher present is not the same as advising a patient at home to walk. The size of the questionnaire change should not be quoted as the effect a British ward would obtain.

## Shift work

### American guidance on shift work disorder, and the review behind it

Wright KP, Abbott SM, Ashbrook L, Drake CL, Flynn-Evans E, Framnes-DeBoer S, et al. Management of shift work disorder: an American Academy of Sleep Medicine clinical practice guideline. *Journal of Clinical Sleep Medicine*. 24 October 2026;22(1):202. [doi:10.1007/s44470-026-00084-w](https://doi.org/10.1007/s44470-026-00084-w). PubMed 42925456.

Wright KP, Abbott SM, Ashbrook L, Drake CL, Flynn-Evans E, Framnes-DeBoer S, et al. Management of shift work disorder: an American Academy of Sleep Medicine systematic review, meta-analysis, and GRADE assessment. *Journal of Clinical Sleep Medicine*. 24 October 2026;22(1):201. [doi:10.1007/s44470-026-00076-w](https://doi.org/10.1007/s44470-026-00076-w). PubMed 42925443.

*Findings.* The American Academy of Sleep Medicine guideline makes conditional suggestions only, each compared with no treatment, for adults with shift work disorder. For excessive sleepiness it suggests armodafinil or modafinil taken 30 to 60 minutes before a night shift, which is the only recommendation graded moderate certainty. It also suggests bright light during the night shift, caffeine before or during the shift, a clockwise rather than a counterclockwise rotating roster, a nap before the night shift, a snack or nothing rather than a full meal, caffeine combined with bright light, and a nap combined with caffeine. For disturbed daytime sleep or insomnia it suggests cognitive behavioural therapy for insomnia, melatonin in specified settings (after a night shift, when moving from daytime to night-time sleep, and before a pre-shift nap), ramelteon or another melatonin-receptor agonist, and suvorexant or another dual orexin-receptor antagonist. It suggests against triazolam and other benzodiazepines. For people seeking circadian adaptation to night work it suggests bright light, including with fixed daytime sleep and, in one package, morning glasses that reduce light transmission. It notes that circadian adaptation is not a realistic aim when shifts change often. It does not suggest preferring an 8-hour night shift to a 12-hour night shift. Remarks warn about sleep inertia after naps, driving after melatonin or while wearing dark glasses, and caffeine close to bedtime. Certainty for almost every statement is very low or low. The companion review retrieved 3,045 articles and took analysable data from 118 studies, mixing randomised trials, observational studies, laboratory simulations and field studies of shift workers with and without a formal diagnosis. The review abstract does not print pooled effect sizes.

*Design and statistics.* Guideline development with the Grading of Recommendations Assessment, Development and Evaluation, commissioned by the Academy and approved by its board. Conditional wording is the right match for very low certainty. Counting simulated shift work and undiagnosed shift workers toward a disorder-specific guideline widens the evidence and blurs the population. Without the pooled estimates in the review abstract, a reader of the abstract alone cannot see the size of benefit the task force judged clinically meaningful. Harms, values and resource use are said to have been considered; the numbers are in the full documents, not here.

*Population and UK relevance.* Written for clinicians treating adults with shift work disorder, from an American professional body. Shift work is common in the National Health Service and in British industry. Licensing, the place of modafinil, and the National Institute for Health and Care Excellence position are British decisions, which this guideline does not make. The certainty grades are the part that travels most cleanly: aside from the wakefulness-promoting medicines, the task force itself rates the evidence as low or very low.

## Sleep, heart disease and mortality

### A multidimensional sleep score in UK Biobank

Wei Z, Xiao Q, Wang S, Liu L, Meng D, Mao Z, et al. Multidimensional sleep health: risk of cardiovascular disease and mortality, and mediation by plasma proteins. *Journal of Global Health*. 23 October 2026;16:04349. [doi:10.7189/jogh.16.04349](https://doi.org/10.7189/jogh.16.04349). PubMed 42924631.

*Findings.* In 257,509 UK Biobank participants, including 26,984 with plasma proteomics, a seven-component sleep health score from the baseline questionnaire was related to later coronary heart disease, stroke, atrial fibrillation, heart failure, cardiovascular death and death from any cause. A higher score was associated with lower risk of each outcome, most strongly, in the authors’ wording, for heart failure and coronary heart disease. Clustered patterns described as mixed sleep disturbance and as non-restorative sleep were associated with higher risks. Linear regression linked the score to 188 proteins, enriched for inflammatory, immune and chemotactic pathways. A shrinkage regression then picked representative proteins, and exploratory mediation analyses found 34 proteins with possible mediation signals. Hepatocyte growth factor, the insulin-like growth factor 2 receptor and fatty-acid-binding protein 4 recurred across outcomes. The abstract does not give hazard ratios, confidence intervals, or the seven components of the score, and it does not give age or sex.

*Design and statistics.* Prospective cohort with Cox models, clustering, protein regression and mediation analysis. The absence of hazard ratios in the abstract is a serious limit on what can be said about the size of the risks. Mediation through proteins is labelled exploratory. A questionnaire score at one baseline visit does not capture later change in sleep, treatment, or shift work. The abstract does not describe missing questionnaire or protein data. Confounding by illness that both disturbs sleep and raises cardiovascular risk is the standing threat to this design, and the abstract says the models were multivariable without listing the covariates.

*Population and UK relevance.* This is a United Kingdom cohort, and it is a volunteer cohort rather than a random sample of adults. People who joined UK Biobank have tended to be healthier and more affluent than the country as a whole, so the associations inform British research on sleep and cardiovascular prevention. They are not clinic prognosis for a patient who has been referred with sleep apnoea, and the abstract gives no basis for ordering these proteins.

## Narcolepsy and rapid eye movement sleep behaviour

### A simple pre-REM marker on an ordinary night study

Mashita M, Adachi H, Mamiya Y, Nakano N, Fujimura A, Mukai M, et al. Modified sleep stage sequence analysis based on the stage immediately preceding each REM sleep period during nocturnal polysomnography to identify candidates for narcolepsy workup. *Sleep Medicine*. 24 October 2026;149:109331. [doi:10.1016/j.sleep.2026.109331](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.sleep.2026.109331). PubMed 42925697.

*Findings.* Overnight studies from 39 people with narcolepsy type 1, 63 with narcolepsy type 2, 62 with idiopathic hypersomnia and 40 healthy controls were re-read. Ignoring sleep-onset rapid eye movement periods, each remaining rapid eye movement period was classed by the single 30-second epoch just before it. The proportion preceded by wake or stage N1 was highest in type 1 narcolepsy and stepped down across type 2, idiopathic hypersomnia and controls. Adding that proportion to rapid eye movement latency raised the median validation area under the curve, over 50 random 70/30 splits, from 0.64 (interquartile range 0.63–0.67) to 0.70 (0.68–0.74) for separating the two narcolepsy types from hypersomnia plus controls. Two high-specificity rules were rounded off: rapid eye movement latency under 40 minutes (median sensitivity 23%, specificity 98%), and a wake-or-N1 proportion of at least 0.4 together with latency under 125 minutes (sensitivity 37%, specificity 97%). The abstract does not give age or sex.

*Design and statistics.* Retrospective diagnostic study with internal validation only. An area under the curve of 0.70 is modest. The rules are built to be specific, so they miss most people who have narcolepsy. They were derived and checked in the same centre’s splits, which is weaker than a second-centre test. The multiple sleep latency test comparison is described only as the same ordering of groups. The abstract does not say how missing or unscorable epochs were handled.

*Population and UK relevance.* A university sleep centre in Osaka. The marker uses a routine night study, which British laboratories already record. Sensitivity this low means a negative rule would not clear someone of narcolepsy. It is a research observation about who might be prioritised for a fuller work-up, not a replacement for the multiple sleep latency test.

### Chin-muscle atonia index for rapid eye movement sleep behaviour disorder

Mogavero MP, Lanza G, Antoci A, Figorilli M, Congiu P, Meloni F, et al. REM Atonia Index Cutoffs for REM Sleep Without Atonia: A Systematic Review with Meta-analysis of Adult Diagnostic Performance. *Sleep*. 19 October 2026. Advance article. [doi:10.1093/sleep/zsag271](https://doi.org/10.1093/sleep/zsag271). PubMed 42895952.

*Findings.* A review of automated rapid eye movement atonia index studies, updated in PubMed/MEDLINE through 27 July 2026 and registered retrospectively on 20 August 2026, included 30 unique studies. Eight adult studies entered a diagnostic-accuracy meta-analysis. Thresholds ranged from 0.80 to 0.90. Pooled sensitivity was 0.850 (95% confidence interval 0.766–0.908) and specificity 0.895 (0.814–0.944). In eight studies the index was lower in rapid eye movement sleep behaviour disorder by a mean difference of −0.263 (−0.335 to −0.190). Only two adult studies offered physiological reference limits, 0.85 and 0.91, and they were not pooled. Paediatric evidence was kept separate. The authors’ conclusion is that the adult chin index discriminates established disease well, and that current evidence does not support one universal cut-off for rapid eye movement sleep without atonia. They advise reporting the index as a continuous number.

*Design and statistics.* Systematic review with a bivariate random-effects model for sensitivity and specificity, which is the appropriate summary for diagnostic studies. Retrospective registration means the protocol was lodged after the search. Thresholds differed across studies, so the pooled accuracy is an average across cut-offs rather than the performance of one rule. Two reference-limit studies cannot define a normal range. The abstract does not report heterogeneity statistics or whether the studies avoided using the index itself to make the reference diagnosis.

*Population and UK relevance.* Adult diagnostic studies, led from an Italian research institute, with an American co-author among others. Sleep laboratories in the United Kingdom that already use this automated index can take the pooled accuracy as a summary of published performance, and the authors’ own warning against a single cut-off is the clinical message. It does not establish a British reference range.

### Cerebral blood-flow regulation from loss of atonia to Parkinson’s disease

Zhang Y, Piao Y, Sun Q, Li H, Wang Y, Lu J, et al. Impaired Dynamic Cerebral Autoregulation Across the REM Sleep Behavior Disorder Spectrum: Evidence From Isolated REM Sleep Without Atonia to Parkinson's Disease With RBD. *CNS Neuroscience and Therapeutics*. October 2026;32(10):e71190. PubMed gives the month, not the day. The record entered PubMed on 22 October 2026. [doi:10.1002/cns.71190](https://doi.org/10.1002/cns.71190). PubMed 42905881.

*Findings.* A case-control study compared 25 people with isolated rapid eye movement sleep without atonia, 30 with isolated behaviour disorder, 28 with Parkinson’s disease plus behaviour disorder, and 30 age- and sex-matched healthy controls, using video-polysomnography, magnetic resonance imaging and transcranial Doppler. The percentage of rapid eye movement sleep was lower in the isolated behaviour-disorder group and the Parkinson’s group than in controls. Phasic muscle activity, and not tonic activity, differed across the three patient groups (p < 0.001). The phase difference used to describe dynamic cerebral autoregulation was lower in all three patient groups than in controls (p < 0.001) and was associated with the amount of rapid eye movement sleep and with phasic muscle activity (both p < 0.001). The abstract does not give the size of those associations, or the ages.

*Design and statistics.* Cross-sectional case-control study with matching on age and sex. A shared reduction in the vascular measure, already present in the isolated loss-of-atonia group, is the main claim. The abstract reports p values without coefficients or confidence intervals, so the size of the vascular difference is not available here. The associations with sleep stage and muscle activity are not described as adjusted for age, sex or blood pressure. One night of polysomnography and one daytime vascular recording cannot show that the vascular change came first. The trial registration number in the abstract (NCT02752139) dates from long before this analysis.

*Population and UK relevance.* A hospital sleep and stroke service in Changchun, China. Rapid eye movement sleep behaviour disorder is part of United Kingdom neurology and sleep practice, including as a prodrome of Parkinson’s disease. These Doppler findings are a research observation. They are not a test to add to a routine British work-up, and the age and sex of the sample are not stated beyond the matching.

## Restless legs syndrome

### A perspective on which intravenous iron to use

Phan DM. Beyond the neurological horizon: A metabolic paradigm shift in intravenous iron therapy for restless legs syndrome. *Sleep Medicine*. 23 October 2026;149:109324. [doi:10.1016/j.sleep.2026.109324](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.sleep.2026.109324). PubMed 42925698.

*Findings.* This is a single-author perspective, not a new patient series and not a meta-analysis. The author argues that ferric carboxymaltose has become a preferred intravenous iron for restless legs syndrome because of its effect on brain iron and its convenience, and that this choice underweights a risk of fibroblast growth factor 23-mediated hypophosphataemia, with osteomalacia, fractures and muscle weakness described from haematology literature. The piece urges a shift toward ferric derisomaltose, ferumoxytol or low-molecular-weight iron dextran, using a risk-stratified choice. The abstract contains no sample size, no effect estimate and no confidence interval.

*Design and statistics.* There is no analysis to appraise. The clinical claims rest on literature the abstract does not quantify. A perspective can raise a safety question. It cannot, by itself, estimate how often the metabolic complication occurs in people treated for restless legs.

*Population and UK relevance.* No patient sample. Intravenous iron is part of specialist restless-legs practice in the United Kingdom. Product choice, monitoring of phosphate, and licensing sit with British regulators, local formularies and the individual prescriber. This article does not restate National Institute for Health and Care Excellence guidance.

## Bruxism, including sleep bruxism

Three adult papers with a publication date inside 19–26 October 2026 deal with bruxism directly. A statement on botulinum toxin for orofacial pain, from the International Network for Orofacial Pain and Related Disorders Methodology, was indexed on 22 October 2026 (*Cranio*, [doi:10.1080/08869634.2026.2743785](https://doi.org/10.1080/08869634.2026.2743785), PubMed 42910774). Its abstract discusses temporomandibular disorders and does not discuss sleep bruxism, so it is not appraised here. A Canadian Community Health Survey analysis linked self-rated oral health, oral pain, bleeding gums and dry mouth with short sleep, insomnia symptoms, unrefreshing sleep and difficulty staying awake (*PLOS One*, 21 October 2026, [doi:10.1371/journal.pone.0350313](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0350313), PubMed 42905758). The abstract does not give the sample size or the confidence intervals, so it is not given a full appraisal.

### Sleep bruxism and awake bruxism at age 40 in the Pelotas birth cohort

Lamaison C, Daneris ÂP, Klumb V, Demarco FF, Horta BL, Boscato N, et al. Sleep and Awake Bruxism and Mental Health Conditions: A Cross-Sectional Analysis of the 1982 Pelotas Birth Cohort. *Journal of Dentistry*. 25 October 2026. Online ahead of print. [doi:10.1016/j.jdent.2026.107135](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jdent.2026.107135). PubMed 42927863.

*Findings.* Members of the 1982 Pelotas birth cohort in southern Brazil were asked, at age 40, about sleep bruxism and awake bruxism. Common mental-disorder symptoms had been measured with the Self-Reporting Questionnaire at ages 30 and 40, and specific disorders with the Mini International Neuropsychiatric Interview at age 40. Both forms of bruxism were more prevalent in women (prevalence ratio 1.48, 95% confidence interval 1.27–1.72, for awake bruxism; 1.47, 1.27–1.70, for sleep bruxism) and in people with alcohol dependence (1.47, 1.09–1.99, and 1.34, 1.00–1.79). Depression was associated with awake bruxism (1.50, 1.10–2.05) and sleep bruxism (1.41, 1.05–1.90). People with probable common mental disorder at both 30 and 40 had a higher prevalence of awake bruxism (2.02, 1.62–2.52) and sleep bruxism (1.52, 1.23–1.88) than people who had never screened positive. The objective mentions long-term prevalence, but the results text in the abstract does not state what percentage reported either form of bruxism, and it does not state how many cohort members answered.

*Design and statistics.* Cross-sectional analysis inside a birth cohort, using Poisson regression with robust variance, crude and adjusted. Prevalence ratios are the right scale for a common outcome. The bruxism measure is a subject report, not an examination or a sleep study, so the ratios describe reported grinding rather than polysomnographic rhythmic masticatory muscle activity. The analysed head-count is missing from the abstract, which blocks any judgement of precision beyond the printed intervals. The alcohol-dependence interval for sleep bruxism touches 1.00. Adjustment variables are described only as demographics, socioeconomic status, mental-health history and unhealthy behaviours. A cross-sectional model cannot show that depression or alcohol dependence produced the grinding.

*Population and UK relevance.* Adults aged 40 in a southern Brazilian city, followed since birth. A general-population cohort is a better frame than a dental clinic, and it is still not the adult population of the United Kingdom. The pattern, more reported grinding where mood disorder has been present for years, is plausible in British primary care. The ratios should not be quoted as British prevalence ratios, and the missing percentage should not be filled in from elsewhere.

### Milled versus 3D-printed stabilisation splints

Rabel K, Linke M, Linke T, Recca M, Lüchtenborg J, Burkhardt F, et al. Clinical Evaluation of 3D-Printed Stabilization Splints for Probable Bruxism: An Exploratory Cohort Analysis of a Randomized Prospective Single-Blinded Crossover Trial. *Journal of Dentistry*. 24 October 2026. Online ahead of print. [doi:10.1016/j.jdent.2026.107133](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jdent.2026.107133). PubMed 42926006.

*Findings.* Seventeen people with probable bruxism, diagnosed from the history and examination with the German Bruxism Screening Index, wore a milled splint and a 3D-printed splint in random order for three months each. The abstract reports no significant difference in oral health-related quality of life, wear of the opposing teeth, wear of the splint, or retention. Printed splints needed less adjustment at insertion and were preferred by most participants for comfort. The authors attribute the comfort and adjustment findings to the splint design used in the trial rather than to the manufacturing method, and they say longer trials with designs matched to each method are still needed. The abstract gives no means, no p values and no count for “most”. Age and sex are not stated.

*Design and statistics.* Exploratory analysis of a randomised, prospective, single-blind crossover. Seventeen completers can miss a moderate difference, so “no significant difference” is not evidence that the two splints are equivalent. Preference and chairside adjustment are real practical outcomes, and they are reported without numbers. Carry-over from the first three months into the second is a standard crossover risk, and the abstract does not describe a wash-out. Probable bruxism on a screening index is not sleep-study bruxism. Blinding of a device the patient can see and feel is limited; the abstract says the trial was single-blind.

*Population and UK relevance.* Adults treated in a prosthetic dentistry department in Freiburg. A British dental service that is choosing between milled and printed splints may find the short-term feasibility useful. The sample is too small, and the follow-up too short, to prefer one manufacturing route for patients with sleep bruxism.

### Experimental jaw bracing and clenching in healthy young adults

Iwata Y, Yoshida K, Kothari SF, Iida T, Devendran A, Ishii Y, et al. Effect of repeated bouts of bracing and clenching jaw activities on musculoskeletal symptoms in healthy young individuals. *Journal of Prosthodontic Research*. 23 October 2026. Online ahead of print. [doi:10.2186/jpr.JPR_D_25_00397](https://doi.org/10.2186/jpr.JPR_D_25_00397). PubMed 42927900.

*Findings.* Thirty healthy young adults performed, on each of four days, nine five-minute bouts of either bracing or clenching, with one minute of rest between bouts. Fatigue scores on a 0–10 visual analogue scale were higher just after the tasks than before them and higher than at 24 hours, on each day (p < 0.05). Fatigue scores were also higher than pain and headache scores (p < 0.05) on all days for both tasks, except for pain after clenching on day 4 (p = 0.196). The authors read this as short-lived fatigue with little pain, and they suggest that longer-lasting pain needs risk factors beyond repetitive jaw loading. The abstract does not give mean scores, age, sex, or any sleep recording.

*Design and statistics.* Experimental within-person study in healthy volunteers, not a trial of treatment and not a study of sleep. Four days of standardised loading cannot represent years of bruxism. Multiple day-by-day comparisons are reported at a p < 0.05 threshold without a stated correction. The one non-significant pain comparison on day 4 is a reminder that the fatigue-versus-pain contrast was not uniform. Electromyography was collected; the abstract’s conclusion does not rest on a numeric electromyographic result.

*Population and UK relevance.* Healthy young volunteers. The first author is in Matsudo, Japan. People who attend a United Kingdom clinic with painful sleep bruxism are older on average and are already symptomatic. The experiment speaks to jaw-muscle resilience in healthy adults. It does not estimate the prevalence of sleep bruxism and it does not test a splint, a medicine or a behavioural treatment.

## What this week does not settle

The in-window adult literature is led by one large industry-funded drug trial and by a great deal of observational and single-centre work. Retatrutide reduced the apnoea–hypopnoea index more than placebo at 80 weeks in adults with overweight or obesity, and the abstract leaves the gastrointestinal event rates, the sex balance and the countries of enrolment unstated. The Santander telemonitoring series places treatment-emergent central apnoea at 4.3%, mostly in the first two months. The UK Biobank sleep-score paper is the most directly British result, and its abstract does not give the hazard ratios. The American shift-work guideline is deliberately conditional, with moderate certainty reserved for armodafinil or modafinil and with very low or low certainty for almost everything else.

Adult bruxism this week is a birth-cohort survey that does not print its prevalence, a 17-person splint crossover, and a four-day loading experiment in healthy volunteers. None of these should change British dental or sleep-clinic practice on its own.

A Spanish survey of 1,898 adults aged 40 to 60, published on 22 October 2026, found that links between night-time road-traffic noise and sleep differed by city, and the abstract does not print the regression coefficients (*Environmental Research*, [doi:10.1016/j.envres.2026.125987](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.envres.2026.125987), PubMed 42914074). It was not given a full appraisal for that reason. Paediatric papers, animal work, letters, editorials, protocols and preprints were excluded.

## Five lines for a non-specialist reader

This week’s new adult sleep papers were read from PubMed, because Embase asked for an Elsevier sign-in and Scopus stopped at the same publisher’s identity check.
The largest new treatment study found that a weekly injection called retatrutide reduced sleep apnoea more than a placebo in adults with excess weight, over 80 weeks, in trials paid for by the manufacturer.
A Spanish pressure-therapy service found central apnoea in about four in a hundred patients, mostly in the first two months, and a UK Biobank analysis linked healthier sleep patterns with lower risks of heart disease and death without printing the size of those risks in the abstract.
An American shift-work guideline offers only cautious, mostly low-certainty suggestions, and the new tooth-grinding papers are a survey of 40-year-olds in Brazil and a 17-person German splint comparison.
None of this, on its own, is a reason to change usual National Health Service care this week; licensing, British guidelines and the individual patient still govern treatment.

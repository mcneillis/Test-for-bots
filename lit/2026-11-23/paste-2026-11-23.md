# Sleep literature, 16–23 November 2026

Plain-English notes from PubMed abstracts. Not medical advice. Embase and Scopus were not searched.

- PubMed only, 23 November 2026. Entry-date search: 372. Publication-date search: 194. Overlap: 180. Unique: 386. Two papers already in the 9 or 16 November packs were removed. Screened: 384. Included: 24. The week was busy enough to keep this window.
- No paper on sleep-related painful erections. Two acronym hits were about effort ratings in sport, not erections.
- Narcolepsy: in five short trials (550 people), orexin-2 agonist tablets improved wakefulness and cataplexy and caused more side effects. A separate summary says oveporexton was approved in 2026 in China, the United States and Japan. That summary does not report a UK licence.
- Insomnia: across 26 trials (6,353 adults), digital programmes improved insomnia scores. Which app is best is uncertain. After stroke or brain injury, cognitive behavioural therapy for insomnia produced a modest drop in insomnia scores. The depression result in that review was not statistically clear.
- Sleep apnoea: overnight breathing measures favoured CPAP over a jaw-advancement device, and nightly use favoured the jaw device (18 studies, 1,244 people). A telemedicine airway-pressure trial (344 people) was non-inferior to in-person care for 3-month blood pressure, within a 1.5 mmHg margin.
- UK cohorts: irregular sleep was linked with later hypertension in UK Biobank (55,572 people). That paper is a preprint and is not peer-reviewed. Night-time light was linked with small differences in heart structure and with later heart and stroke events, also in UK Biobank. In Whitehall II, poorer sleep–wake profiles were linked with dementia (3,973 older adults).
- Older adults: pooled daytime sleepiness was 16.9% after age 60, with wide geographic spread. In a records study of dementia, dual orexin sleeping tablets were linked with fewer coded falls and delirium episodes than benzodiazepines or Z-drugs. That is not a trial, and the differences are large enough to treat carefully.
- Bruxism: jaw-muscle stiffness was higher in bruxism; muscle thickness was not. In 94 adults who had both sleep apnoea and sleep bruxism, most grinding episodes sat close in time to a breathing event.
- Also indexed, not written up in full: a narrative restless-legs update, an antenatal sleep trial (100 pregnant women with obesity), and a vestibular-stimulation insomnia review of five small trials.
- Abstracts only. No full risk-of-bias scores. Figures are those printed in the abstracts.

## Amendment summary

On 24 October 2026 Dr McNeillis decided to add heart rate from the CBT-14 chest patch to Lazoo’s intended purpose, and that the Lazoo app is the companion to that patch. Later the same day, and again on 26 October 2026, she settled how that heart rate is recorded, who sees it, what a shared report may hold, and what happens in pregnancy. She also decided that a core temperature estimate using the model published by Buller and colleagues in 2013 is to be shown on the phone, but only once the way of showing it has been chosen, the estimate has been validated for that use, and Compliance has reviewed that choice again. The validation protocol still describes a diary-only study. Its wearable fields are empty, it posts no devices, and it has no heart-rate endpoint. The protocol therefore has to say what the product now is, and it has to say, just as clearly, that this study does not yet use that heart-rate function.

The amendment brings the protocol into line with the purpose she has decided, and it states that the study remains a comparison of sleep diaries. She has approved an amendment to the statement of purpose in principle. The final words are still for her to approve, and Compliance has not finished its review. This draft uses her decisions and treats the purpose statement as still unlocked.

The passages affected are these. The intended-purpose position on the cover changes. The product rows in the synopsis and in section 1 change. The device paragraph in section 2 changes. The alignment table in section 3 changes, including the pregnancy row. The scope paragraph in section 4 changes, and the primary objective is quoted and kept. Sections 5 and 6 gain a limit on the chest patch, beside the existing actigraphy rule and the rule that no device is posted. Exclusion criterion 7 in section 8 is quoted and kept, with a note on how the product behaves in pregnancy. Sections 9 and 10 change the sentences on what the app shows, what a share contains, and the empty wearable fields. The actigraphy row in section 11 is quoted and kept, and no wearing row is added. Section 12 keeps the diary endpoint and the feasibility floor, and states that heart rate is not an endpoint. Section 13 adds the handling of heart rate, with no alarm and no threshold. Section 14 keeps the diary export and states that heart rate is not in it. Section 16 changes the purpose sentence and the amendments sentence. Section 19 is quoted so that any later public involvement covers this limit. Section 21 adds risks. Section 22 adds a paragraph on heart rate as health data without changing the diary basis. Section 24 stops the paper and the lay summary from implying a heart-rate result. The version history gains a proposed row and the earlier rows stay. Consequences for the case report form, the data management plan, the consent choices and the participant information sheet are listed only, and those documents are not rewritten here.

## Decisions this draft follows

Dr McNeillis decided at 16:03 on 24 October 2026 to add heart rate to Lazoo’s intended purpose. The Lazoo app is the companion to CBT-14. The patch is worn on the left side of the chest. The fuller description already on the record is the lower lateral chest wall, about 20 centimetres below the armpit fold, on the left side. The level on the ribs remains with the fit study. Both temperature sensors face the skin. The claim for the patch stays at skin temperature trends. Logging of blood oxygen saturation waits until it has been bench tested, and it is not part of this study. She has accepted heart rate in Lazoo as a medical device function.

At 17:07 the same day she decided that heart rate is recorded across the whole day. Her reason is that heart rate together with core temperature is essential for reading the daily rhythm. That rhythm is context for insomnia monitoring. Lazoo does not diagnose a circadian rhythm disorder. Heart rate is worked out on the phone. On 26 October 2026 she decided that the shared report holds only the recorded data. The recorded heart rate can be in that report, which is the sharing she had already decided, and it leaves the phone only when the person chooses to share. The core temperature estimate stays on the phone and stays out of the report. She decided that the estimate is to be shown on the phone, including in pregnancy, and that in pregnancy the raw heart rate is hidden and the estimate is not. No core temperature figure, curve or label is shown to anyone until she has chosen how it is shown, the estimate has been validated for that use, and Compliance has reviewed that choice again. Her words at 23:30 on 26 October 2026 were “Number from heart rate is fine”. The intended-purpose amendment reads that sentence as the pregnancy answer: the estimate derived from heart rate stays visible on the phone, and only the raw heart rate is hidden. It is not a choice among a pattern of timing, a band against the person’s own record, or a figure in degrees. A band would be a threshold. The guards allow a threshold only if it is redesigned and reviewed again. How the estimate is shown remains open for her.

Dated note, 29 October 2026: decision 2 of the CBT-14 addendum widens the data-path lock of 18 September 2026 so that heart rate travels to the app. Under Dr McNeillis's answer 4 of 24 October 2026, that wider flow starts only after the data protection impact assessment (CBT-14-HR-DPIA-2026-10-24, version 0.5.1, sha256 41861d51…, a draft passed for sign-off, not approved) is signed off and the purpose amendment LAZ-IP-001-AMD-01 is approved.
